Paper by paper

P1

How Reproducible Is a Guard Evaluation? A Measured Floor, and Where It Isn't Small

Source of truth: the 18 per-item run files on Modal volume warrantor-p1, recomputed 2026-09-05. Eight arm-A runs, six thread-count runs, four hardware runs, 1,000 items each.

What the record showed that the paper did not say
  • The two unstable items flip together, in the same runs. Items m0489 and m0749 each return their minority verdict in exactly two of eight arm-A runs (A3 and A7), not "roughly half the time" as the paper had it: twelve disagreeing pairs is a 2-versus-6 split. Across all eighteen runs the same two items are the only unstable ones, they flip in four runs, and always together. Every run is in one of two states. The unit of instability is the run, not the item.
  • Every flip crosses the Safe/Controversial boundary. Neither item ever moves between Safe and Unsafe. The whole measured floor is a property of the model's undecided class. Under the alternative policy that scores Controversial as safe, one of the two would not count, so the floor would be one item, and which item is unstable would depend on a policy setting.
  • Arms B and C inherit the same structure. Their only disagreements are the same two items, with one run in each arm sitting in the minority state.
Added
  • Wilson 95% interval on the per-item instability rate, [0.05%, 0.73%], in the abstract and results table; the "differences above 0.1% are real" claim tempered accordingly.
  • The companion floor on a second corpus (P8: 0.107%, also exactly two unstable items), cited by DOI, sharpening the population hypothesis to "the floor lives in the undecided class".
  • A serving-stack paragraph disclosing that the Ollama/llama.cpp version was never recorded, stated as the gap the paper's own §6 asks others to close.
  • The Artifact section and the prior-art scope note, neither of which had reached the v1 or v2 PDF: both sat after the References heading, which the builder treats as the end of the body. Moved into the body.
  • Threats to validity: the items were inspected at verdict level; four of eighteen runs in the minority state is itself a small sample (Wilson [9%, 45%]).
Removed
  • An uncited LongGuard reference entry.
Verified: 998/1000 unanimous, mean pairwise agreement 0.99914, worst pair 0.998, 16 identical pairs, arm B 0.9993 both ways, arm C 1.000 / 0.998 / 0.999. All match the published figures; the structure above is what they hid.
P3

Positional Guard Evasion at Short Context: A Replication Note

Source of truth: per-item verdicts on volumes warrantor-p3 (1,000 inputs × 2 models) and warrantor-p3x (1,260 × 2), recomputed 2026-09-05.

Corrected
  • A novelty claim inside a replication note. §5.1 said the positional profile in a safety classifier was "to our reading, new" three sections after the note declared itself a replication of LongGuard. Now attributed.
  • "Holds at every individual length in both models." It does not: the 0.6B's 1× cell has start above middle (6 versus 5 of 100). Only the 4B's per-length table had been shown. The 0.6B table is added and the sentence states the one exception.
  • A lowercase sentence start left by the previous revision; the conclusion's "at every length, in both scales" restated to match.
Added
  • Paired tests that respect the design. The pooled 300-item cells count each payload three times, so the Fisher tests were on dependent data. Matched-cell McNemar: the 4B is missed in the middle and caught at the end in 30 of 30 discordant cells (p = 9.3 × 10⁻¹⁰), 26 of 26 against start; the 0.6B 19:1 and 18:5. Risk ratios with 95% intervals (4.33 [2.14, 8.79] and 2.64 [1.34, 5.18]). Only 21 of 100 (4B) and 16 of 100 (0.6B) payloads are ever missed.
  • P3-X made explicit. Which 60 payloads (the first 60 by identifier, a budget decision), the five positions, and the payload-level unit that reproduces the reported 19:0 and 2:4 counts exactly ("missed at any position"). The 4B's individual steps 1k→4k and 16k→32k do not reach significance on that test; the endpoints carry the inference. Payloads ever missed rise to 30 of 60 and 28 of 60.
  • The 0.6B control moved from 0/100 to 1/60 when num_ctx was raised to 32768, noted as the only control-cell movement in the paper.
  • LongGuard's chunked-detection defense named as the existing implementation of the follow-up §5.3 proposes.
  • A dependence threat in §6.
T04

Masking a Field's Loss Does Not Isolate That Field

Source of truth: hf/guard-verdicts/runs.json run identities, the T04-X2 stage-2 records on volume warrantor-t04x2-evals, the training recipe constants, and the paper's own revision note of 2026-08-31.

Corrected
  • "Arm A is a retrain of run 1's configuration." Run 1 trained on the 38,694-row mixture and was scored on WildGuardTest; arm A trains on an 11,272-row corpus and reproduces the ExpGuard run of 2026-08-22 (unsafe 1038, safe 1237, zero Controversial in the record). §5.1 and §5.2 now say so, and the corpus difference is flagged.
  • "The mechanistic claim of §7" pointed at Limitations; it is §4.3.
  • One British spelling ("cancelled") that the US-English verifier flagged.
Added
  • The pre-registered follow-up T04-X2 (2a223cac), which the paper's own revision note had written the paragraphs for and the paper never received. On Aegis, through transformers, at both scales, from a format-competent checkpoint: masking the severity line moved its distribution significantly (χ² 10.48 and 14.67, df 2), and all 111 moved verdicts went Unsafe→Safe while emission held at 99.8%. Stage-2 counts verified from the records: 379/817 (0.6B) and 426/771 (4B). Only the Unsafe-down half of the direction claim was testable, since Aegis is two-valued.
  • The failure mode found on the way to it (T04-X): masking a field during format-acquisition SFT annihilates it, 0 of 1,199 across eleven configurations, with the supervised field degraded alongside.
  • The adapter hyperparameters (rank 16, alpha 32, dropout 0.05, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴, batch 2 × accumulation 8) in §2; the gate's comparator (the 4B baseline, 0.8554) in §3.1.
  • A third masked-severity run with retained per-item outputs (2026-08-22, local): Controversial 231 versus 49, Unsafe 386 versus 650, category words in the severity slot, recall 0.7374. The run 2 signature at different magnitudes, from verdicts that are in the released dataset.
  • §4.3 now says the weight-sharing account is mechanistic and that the separate-adapter positive control is unrun.
  • A cross-reference to P1 in §3.3: the class the lever acts on is where the measured floor lives, so a fine-tune that removes the class removes the floor with the control.
