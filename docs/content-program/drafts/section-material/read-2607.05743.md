## 1. What it establishes

**Thesis.** Execution security for coding agents — the boundary between an agent's decision and the operating
system — is a subject with its own internal structure, but the literature addressing it is fragmented across
non-citing sub-literatures. Four existing broader agentic-security surveys treat sandboxing as one entry in a
wide catalog; none organizes execution security on its own terms. This paper is written to fill that gap and
claims to be **"the first, to our knowledge, to treat execution security as a subject with its own internal
structure."**

**Exact organizing principle — three nested lenses, not one.** This matters for our positioning, and our
current §1.1 table understates it:

1. **Primary axis (Section IV, Table I): 17 categories by the execution-security *mechanism* a paper
   proposes or measures.** Single-category assignment — each paper lands in exactly one. Categories and
   counts: isolation architectures (5), escape and adversarial benchmarks (6), access control and capability
   models (6), policy enforcement fragility (2), TOCTOU (2), MCP-specific threats and defenses (4),
   systems-security framing (2), harness-level capability evaluation (1), identity and credential delegation
   (2), execution provenance and auditability (1), network egress control (1), static analysis of
   agent-generated code (1), scope-creep and overeager-action measurement (1), skill and plugin packaging
   security (1), prompt-injection foundations (2), jailbreak and alignment-attack foundations (1), agent
   safety-awareness evaluation (1). Total 39.
2. **Second lens (Section V-A, Table II): four root causes.** RC1 no structural separation between untrusted
   content and control input; RC2 authorization is checked once and trusted forever; RC3 a mechanism encodes
   whether an action is *permitted*, not whether *this invocation matches intent*; RC4 defenses are validated
   against author-constructed attackers rather than observed real-world failure. Table II maps all 17
   categories onto RC1–RC4. Six categories map to more than one root cause, eight to exactly one, **three map
   to none** (isolation architectures, systems-security framing, jailbreak foundations). No category maps to
   more than two — the paper's stated conclusion is that no existing mechanism fixes more than half the
   recurring defects.
3. **Third lens (Section V-B, Table III): pipeline stage × role.** Stage is one of pre-action, at-action,
   post-action, post-hoc; role is one of mechanism, measurement, foundational, perspective. Eight papers
   pre-action, eight at-action, exactly one post-action mechanism category (execution provenance). Every
   measurement category is post-hoc.
4. **Figure 18:** a 17×17 pairwise category-similarity heatmap, scored as cosine similarity of each
   category's root-cause weight vector plus a bonus for shared dominant pipeline stage.

The paper explicitly labels lenses 2 and 3 as **"our synthesis rather than a new empirical claim… an
interpretive lens, not a replacement"** for the mechanism grouping.

**Five stated gaps (Section VI), substantively verbatim:**

- **Gap 1** — Isolation and access control are not evaluated against each other. None of the 5 isolation
  papers and none of the 6 access-control papers share a benchmark, so a reader cannot learn whether they are
  substitutes or complements.
- **Gap 2** — Defenses are not re-evaluated under known enforcement failure rates. Nobody re-runs the measured
  denylist bypasses against their own mechanism.
- **Gap 3** — TOCTOU and MCP tool poisoning are studied as separate literatures despite both being a
  validate-then-act race; neither literature cites the other or uses its vocabulary.
- **Gap 4** — Every enforcement mechanism assumes an honest policy author. None studies policy-authoring
  error — overly permissive by mistake, internally contradictory, or broad because narrower scoping is more
  work. The paper calls this "the most consequential of the first four."
- **Gap 5** — Scope creep is measured but addressed by no enforcement mechanism. A capability system
  constrains *what an agent is allowed to do*, not *whether an authorized capability is invoked when it
  should not be*; the literature does not distinguish the two problems.

## 2. Method and scale

- **Search:** iterative arXiv and web keyword search (sandbox, execution isolation, agent harness, coding
  agent, capability, access control, TOCTOU, Model Context Protocol), expanded as new author groups surfaced.
  Self-described as **"iterative and citation-following rather than exhaustive."**
- **Inclusion:** addresses the execution layer of an LLM agent, posted January 2023 to July 2026. Broader
  surveys are excluded from the taxonomy and handled in Related Work.
- **Verification protocol (Appendix B) — this is the load-bearing weakness.** For each candidate the author
  fetched the paper's own abstract page and confirmed **title, author list, year, venue, and one-sentence
  contribution** against the drafted citation. Two misattributions were caught and corrected. The paper then
  states plainly that the protocol *"does not constitute independent replication of any paper's empirical
  claims; we report those claims as stated by their authors."*
- **Reproducibility (Section III-D, Appendix D):** every count in the prose is mechanically re-derived from a
  released corpus CSV by a verifier script; the corpus, two generators and the verifier ship as a supplement.
  This is genuinely good practice and we should acknowledge it.
- **Corpus composition by year (Table IV):** 2023 → 4, 2024 → 4, 2025 → 14, 2026 → 17.
- **Appendix A, Table V:** full per-paper table — first author, year, venue, arXiv ID, category, root cause,
  stage, role. All 39 arXiv IDs are recoverable, which is what made the overlap analysis below possible.

**Corpus overlap with our 15 scored systems — the single most important finding in this read.**

2602.09433, 2603.17170, 2603.17419, 2603.24775, 2604.18652, 2604.23646, 2605.06393, 2605.20704, 2605.26298,
2605.27488.

**Intersection with their 39: exactly one paper — arXiv:2601.11893 (SEAgent).** One of our fifteen; one of
their thirty-nine.

The "same population" premise in the task brief is **false**. Their sample is weighted toward container
sandboxes, escape benchmarks, MCP server audits, CVEs and foundational injection work. It contains **none** of
the information-flow, capability-DSL and provenance strand that dominates ours. Fourteen of our fifteen scored
systems do not appear anywhere in their 39. The two works are close to independent samples of the same field.

This cuts both ways and we should say so ourselves before a reviewer does: it substantially reduces the
"already systematized" threat, and it raises a coverage-validity question about **both** corpora that we
should pre-empt in our Limitations rather than let a reviewer raise about ours alone.

## 4. Weakest attackable point

Ranked. The first is decisive and checkable.

1. **The corpus misses the entire capability-DSL, information-flow and provenance strand.** Fourteen of our
   fifteen scored systems are absent from all 39 entries. This is not a hedge — it is a demonstrable coverage
   failure in a paper whose central claim is a complete structural reading of the field. Its own Limitations
   concede the search was "iterative and citation-following rather than exhaustive," which makes the
   concession available to us without an ungenerous reading. **Use this once, factually, without adjectives.**
2. **Its verification protocol verifies bibliographic identity, not guarantees.** By its own statement, every
   empirical claim is reported "as stated by their authors." The taxonomy is therefore a well-audited *index
   of what papers say about themselves.* That is exactly the hole adjudication fills. This is the cleanest
   wedge and it is entirely fair, because the paper volunteers it.
3. **Eight of seventeen categories hold exactly one paper**, and the paper itself calls this ambiguous between
   a nascent direction and an incomplete search. A taxonomy where nearly half the bins are singletons is a
   weak partition.
4. **Single-category assignment understates overlap** (self-admitted; SEAgent and FORGE are named as papers
   that straddle). The root-cause lens does not partition either: three of seventeen categories map to none of
   RC1–RC4.
5. **The 39 includes four papers the paper itself places outside its subject** — prompt-injection foundations
   (2), jailbreak foundations (1) and safety-awareness evaluation (1) are labeled inherited foundations with
   no pipeline-stage contribution. The headline count is inflated by roughly 10% relative to the actual
   execution-security population.
6. **Single author, arXiv only, no venue, no peer review claimed.** State this only if the venue matters to
   the framing; do not lead with it.

## 5. What it FORECLOSES for our paper

Every item here is now unavailable as a novelty claim and, where we touch the ground, must be cited to them.

- **Priority on systematizing execution security for coding agents.** Their claim to be first is dated 7 July
  2026 and will stand. We must not claim to be the first systematization of this literature.
- **The fragmentation observation itself** — that these sub-literatures do not cite one another. That is their
  title thesis.
- **"Authorization is checked once and trusted forever" (RC2)** and **"permitted, but not intended now"
  (RC3)**. These are published. Our authorization–execution gap is a *different* gap — it is about the
  **strength of the binding** (a claim verified at cryptographic tier whose action is bounded only at an
  application chokepoint), not about staleness (RC2) or intent mismatch (RC3). We must cite RC2 and RC3 and
  draw the distinction explicitly, or a reviewer will read A-T1/B-T3 as a renaming of RC3.
- **"Defenses are validated against author-constructed attackers" (RC4)** — published.
- **Gap 1 (isolation versus access control never compared), Gap 2 (defenses not re-tested under measured
  fragility), Gap 3 (TOCTOU and MCP tool poisoning as one validate-then-act race), Gap 4 (honest policy
  author), Gap 5 (scope creep unaddressed by access control).** All five are theirs. If any of our findings
  restates one, cite it as confirmation from a disjoint sample — which is a real contribution — rather than as
  discovery.
- **The pre-action / at-action / post-action pipeline framing** (Table III). If any part of our tier-of-action
  column reads as "where in the pipeline," we must distinguish it from stage or we will look derivative.
- **The four CVEs and the Pwn2Own Berlin 2026 coding-agent category** as incident grounding. Cite them.
- **"Only one post-action mechanism in the corpus"** and the mechanism-heavy, evaluation-light diagnosis of
  the field.

## 6. What it LEAVES OPEN

- **Any notion of tier.** No strength-of-binding concept exists in the paper. The two-column split — tier of
  acceptance versus tier of action, weakest-link within each column — is entirely unclaimed.
- **Advertised versus scored.** Their protocol takes stated guarantees at face value by design. "12 of 14
  scorable systems bind lower than advertised" is a class of result their method structurally cannot yield.
  This is our strongest clear-air claim.
- **A frozen effector set and a derived per-system coverage matrix.** No effector enumeration exists there.
  "2 of 105 cells reach covered" and "network egress reached by 14 of 15, bounded by none" are unclaimed —
  though the second should nod to their one-paper egress category.
- **Adversary placement as a scored axis**, primary and secondary, and the derived facts (A5 never primary;
  A-T2 empty). Only Gap 4 touches the A3 cell, and only as honest error.
- **Pairwise composition *adjudication* indexed by placement.** Their Gap 1 poses the question and Section X
  asks for the experiment; Figure 18 scores similarity, not compatibility. "20 of 105 pairs fail, all in the
  A2×A4 cell" *answers* a question they published. Frame it that way — answering a named open problem from a
  disjoint corpus is stronger and safer than claiming to have asked it first.
- **Enforceability and monitorability in the Schneider sense.** Nothing in their paper touches it. "Split on
  50 of 50 codings — a monitorable safety surrogate enforced beneath a non-monitorable advertised objective"
  is wholly open ground.
- **Graded evidence (L0–L3).** They have a binary bibliographic check only.
- **Scoring the authors' own system first, at full severity, under a declared conflict.** They have no system
  in the corpus.
- **The corpus itself.** Fourteen of our fifteen systems are outside their sample.

## 8. Suggested related-work paragraph (third person, double-blind safe)

> The closest systematization appeared in July 2026 [X]: thirty-nine execution-security papers from 2023 to
> 2026, organized into seventeen mechanism categories and re-read against four root causes and a
> pre-/at-/post-action pipeline stage, yielding five cross-cutting gaps — among them that isolation
> architectures and capability models are never evaluated against one another, and that no mechanism paper

> verifies each entry's bibliographic identity and then, by its own statement, reports every empirical claim
> as its authors state it; the present paper takes the complementary step of adjudicating those claims,
> separating the tier at which a claim is verified from the tier at which an action is bounded, deriving
> mediation coverage over a frozen set of seven effectors, and indexing pairwise composition by adversary
> placement. The two corpora are also nearly disjoint: of the fifteen systems scored here, exactly one appears
> in that thirty-nine-paper sample, so the two systematizations are better read as independent samples of one
> field than as competing readings of one sample. Where that work asks whether isolation and access control
> are substitutes or complements, this paper answers for every pair in its corpus and reports the twenty pairs
> for which the answer is no.
