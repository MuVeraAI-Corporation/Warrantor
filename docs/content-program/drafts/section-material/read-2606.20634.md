## 1. What it establishes

**Thesis.** The *container fallacy*: treating the presence of an evidence container (a trace, a ledger, a schema-valid record, a compliance checklist, a regime-native validator pass) as if it established property-level sufficiency for a governance question about a specific decision. The paper names the underlying problem the *Decision Evidence Gap* — no published benchmark measures whether heterogeneous agent-runtime records are sufficient for governance questions about a specific decision.

**What it actually demonstrates.** On a 64-case synthetic corpus with deterministically generated labels:

- trace-present and schema-present baselines each mark 56 of 64 cases sufficient and overclaim on 48 → **Overclaim Rate 0.75**
- ledger-present marks 40 sufficient, overclaims on 32 → **0.50**
- container-checklist and source-specific-validator overclaim on **0** (they mark only the 8 complete cases sufficient)
- the property-level candidate scorer overclaims on **0**, with **mean Property Sufficiency Accuracy 0.5625** (288 of 512 property judgments)
- paired-oracle kappa **1.0** overall and per property family, on 512 paired property labels

**Explicitly disclaimed.** Section 3.1 non-goals: it does not measure legal adequacy or regulatory conformance; does not measure production prevalence; does not score task success; does not certify any scorer as a production audit tool; does not replay hidden model reasoning under altered weights or prompts. Section 8.2 repeats that a benchmark pass cannot certify satisfaction of the EU AI Act, a harmonized standard, a sectoral regulator, or a court's view of sufficient evidence.

**Motivating incident.** AI Incident Database #1433 — the Antigravity 1.0 D-drive wipe (December 2025). Framed as: the damage was concrete, the governance question is whether the decision record behind it can be reconstructed.

## 2. Method and scale

### 2.1 The unit

A case is a tuple: one synthetic scenario, one regime-native evidence bundle, one target decision event, one governance question family, one degradation condition, one declared substitution axis. The scorer sees only the normalized bundle. **Not** an agent task, **not** a trace file, **not** a compliance document.

### 2.2 The eight evidence regimes (3.2)

| # | Regime | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AER | reasoning provenance — intent, observation, inference, plans, verdicts |
| 2 | MAT | message-action contract traces for agentic orchestration |
| 3 | IEEC | intent-to-execution chains (OpenKedge), nine fields |
| 4 | DCC / HDP | signed delegation evidence — chains, human-session provenance, authority scope, parent hashes, signatures |
| 5 | PROV | W3C-style workflow provenance graphs extended to agentic workflows |
| 6 | LLM Audit Trails | lifecycle ledger across selection, fine-tuning, deployment, incident response |
| 7 | AEGIS-NTC | pre-execution tool-call firewall decisions, hash-chain audit records |
| 8 | Dynamic Capabilities replay | capability tokens, cryptographic bindings, signed ledgers, replay anchors, reproducibility attestations |

Each regime has a deterministic **adapter** that translates rather than scores — one linear pass over native records, one normalized fragment each, preserving identifiers, timestamps, object types, validation status, provenance. Critically, the adapter records *why* a property is absent: because the regime never encodes it, or because the transformation removed it. A regime is not penalized for omitting a property it never claimed to encode, unless a baseline overclaims from that omission.

### 2.3 The eight property / governance-question families (3.4) — this is the requested list

1. **Actor identity** — who performed the action
2. **Principal authority** — on whose behalf, and what delegation or approval chain authorized it
3. **Action boundary** — what operation was requested, allowed, blocked, executed, or exceeded
4. **Policy basis** — which rule, contract, or approval bundle applied
5. **Decision basis** — what evidence, reasoning, state, or model output supported the decision
6. **Data-and-resource touch** — which data, tool, file, account, or external resource was accessed or mutated
7. **Lifecycle context** — which model version, prompt version, tool catalog, release manifest, or monitoring phase bound the event
8. **Verification strength** — whether the property is cryptographically bound, schema-validated, replayable, attested, or merely narrated

Vocabulary derives from the author's Decision Event Schema. **Note family 8**: it is the paper's nearest structural analog to a verification-tier column, and it is a graded property *of a record*, not of a mechanism.

**Label set.** Per case and family: sufficient / partial / missing / opaque / conflicting / structurally-unfillable. Case-level sufficiency is a **strict conjunction** — sufficient only when every needed property is sufficient or explicitly out of scope. The scorer-side category set drops "missing" (reported as opaque or structurally-unfillable).

### 2.4 The eight degradation conditions (3.6) — this is the requested list

| Condition | Transformation |
|---|---|
| **complete** | preserves all generated evidence |
| **missing-delegation** | masks principal-authority evidence — DCC/HDP chains, approval identities, parent hashes |
| **missing-policy** | removes policy decision records, evaluated rules, approval-bundle references |
| **missing-context** | removes lifecycle context — model version, prompt version, tool catalog, release manifest, monitoring phase |
| **conflicting-identity** | introduces controlled disagreement among actor, principal, signer, approver, tool-executor fields |
| **partial-graph** | drops provenance edges, delegation hops, lifecycle links, or replay anchors *while preserving enough structure for the container to appear valid* |
| **final-only** | retains the final action or incident artifact, removes intermediate evidence |
| **artifact-only** | retains post-hoc documentation, attestation, or compliance package, removes runtime decision records |

All transformations are deterministic and evaluator-independent, defined over both native and normalized records, with both pre-degradation and degraded bundles published plus a transformation log naming the removed or conflicted field. Degradation is applied before scoring, identically for all scorers.

**Load-bearing for us: these are generator-applied maskings, not adversary actions.** There is no attacker anywhere in the construction.

### 2.5 The four counterfactual substitution axes (3.5)

policy (rule, approval, or contract bundle); cost (budget, reservation, termination envelope); drift (model, prompt, tool catalog, release manifest); cache (state, embedder version, staleness, invalidation). All four are recorded in manifests; the released package exercises only a subset, and unexercised axes must be reported as not evaluated, never folded into claims.

### 2.6 Baselines (4) and metrics (6)

Five deterministic container-presence baselines — trace-present, ledger-present, schema-present, container-checklist (model card plus audit trail plus provenance graph plus compliance package, all present), source-specific-validator (one validator per dominant regime, Table 2, exhaustive over the package, unmapped regimes default to insufficient). An optional LLM-judge extension is specified but **not run**; 7.1 states LLM-judge outputs are absent by design.

Metrics: **Property Sufficiency Accuracy** (per-property correctness, strict view — partial maps to insufficient) and **Overclaim Rate** (case-level sufficient returned while at least one required property is insufficient). Because Overclaim Rate is asymmetric — a constant-insufficient scorer trivially scores zero — the paper adds **Sufficient-claim Rate** and **Underclaim Rate** and reports two trivial anchors: always-insufficient (Overclaim 0.000, Underclaim 1.000) and always-sufficient (Overclaim 0.875, Underclaim 0.000). Secondary metrics defined: Gap Localization F1, Evidence Strength Calibration, Cross-Regime Transfer, Degradation Sensitivity.

### 2.7 Scale, in one line

**64 cases = 8 regimes x 8 degradations, exactly one case per cell.** 512 property judgments (64 x 8 families). Ground truth deliberately imbalanced: 8 strictly sufficient, 56 strictly insufficient. Six scorers (five baselines plus one candidate), zero human annotators, zero LLM judges, zero real captured traces. Whole 64-case run under 5 seconds; end-to-end under 60 seconds per case on a consumer laptop.

### 2.8 The results that matter downstream

**Table 6 (overclaim by degradation).** missing-delegation, missing-policy and conflicting-identity produce 8-of-8 overclaim for all three overclaiming baselines. artifact-only is asymmetric: schema-present overclaims 8 of 8, trace-present and ledger-present do not. partial-graph reverses: schema-present abstains, the other two still overclaim.

**Table 8 (candidate PSA by property family).** data-and-resource touch 0.875; actor identity 0.750; verification strength 0.625; principal authority, policy basis, decision basis and lifecycle context all 0.500; **action boundary 0.250 — the floor.** Section 8.4 names action-boundary scoring the number-one priority for v2, stating the redacted scorer relies on policy- and lifecycle-derived features that do not transfer there.

**Table 7 (candidate PSA by degradation).** complete 1.000 → final-only 0.875 → artifact-only 0.750 → missing-delegation 0.500 → missing-context / missing-policy / partial-graph 0.375 → conflicting-identity 0.250.

## 3. The critical question: does it map sufficiency to a supervisory or regulatory demand?

**Answer: no. It positions against supervisory demand rhetorically; it maps nothing structurally. Sufficiency is defined entirely relative to the author's own property set.**

The evidence, both ways:

**What regulatory content is present.**

- Section 1.3, "Regulatory Substrate," cites EU AI Act Articles 12, 14, 26, 50 and 73 as creating logging, human-oversight, deployer-obligation, transparency and serious-incident-reporting pressure; notes Annex III high-risk toward 2 December 2027, Annex I product-embedded toward 2 August 2028, and Article 50 synthetic-content marking with 2 December 2026 in current planning summaries. It cites OSCAL extension work for machine-readable compliance evidence, Google SynthID as an Article 50 partial, and EN 18228, EN 18282 and Singapore IMDA's Agentic AI Framework as accountability moving toward runtime evidence.
- Section 8.2 names **the joint Federal Reserve / OCC / FDIC revised interagency model risk management guidance of 17 April 2026** as the strongest sectoral comparator. It correctly reports the generative-and-agentic-AI scope carve-out, and explicitly reads it as *not* deregulation: institutions must apply existing risk management and governance practices to systems not covered, and the agencies plan a request for information. It then positions DEMM-Bench as measuring the agent-runtime decision-evidence territory the guidance has declined to specify — the same territory the announced RFI marks — and says the relationship is complementary, not competitive.

**Why that is positioning, not mapping.**

1. **There is no crosswalk anywhere in the paper.** No table, no appendix, no mapping of any of the eight property families or eight question families to an AI Act article, a supervisory examination expectation, a harmonized-standard clause, or an evidentiary standard. The regulatory material appears in exactly two places — an introduction subsection and a discussion subsection — and never touches the construction, the labels, the metrics or the results.
2. **The label semantics are self-referential.** Sufficient means the evidence directly fills *the property for the question*, where both property and question come from the author's Decision Event Schema and DEMM companion preprints. Nothing external adjudicates what a property must bind. The construction oracle is a versioned rule file written by the same author.
3. **The paper says so itself, twice.** Section 3.1 lists legal adequacy and regulatory conformance as non-goals, dependent on jurisdiction, facts and qualified assessment. Section 8.2 states a benchmark pass cannot certify AI Act satisfaction, a harmonized standard, a sectoral regulator, or a court's view of sufficient evidence — that remains a separate assessment by qualified actors. Sections 3.10 and 6.3 repeat the disclaimer at the label and metric layers.
4. **The sole regulatory *inference* is directional, not evidentiary.** The Fed/OCC/FDIC passage argues that because supervisors declined to specify the agent territory, a vendor-neutral measurement object for that territory is useful. That is an argument for the benchmark's existence. It never claims a passing score answers a supervisor's question.

**Consequence for our positioning.** The supervisory-crosswalk lane is **open**. The strongest recent measurement work in adjacent territory has (a) conceded that its property set is its own, (b) named the April 2026 interagency guidance and the announced RFI as the unspecified territory, and (c) built no bridge to it. If our paper carries a defensible mapping from tier-of-action to a supervisory demand — what a supervisor can actually ask for, and what tier of bound would answer it — that is not merely unclaimed; it is the gap this paper points at and declines to enter.

Two cautions. First, DEMM-Bench's discipline here is *correct*, and a reviewer will apply the same discipline to us: any mapping we make must run from *what a demand asks for* to *what a tier establishes*, never to a claim that a tier satisfies a demand. Second, the paper's own MRM reading — deferral, not exemption — is now published prior art; we can no longer present that reading as a novel observation, only cite it and build past it.

## 4. Weakest attackable point

Ranked. The first is the one to use.

**(1) The distinguishing metric has no floor and no comparator.** This is the sharpest, and it is verifiable from the paper's own tables. On every case-level metric the candidate scorer is **indistinguishable** from two of the baselines: Table 5 gives container-checklist, source-specific-validator and the candidate all 8-of-64 sufficient verdicts, 0.000 Overclaim, 0.000 Underclaim, 0.125 Sufficient-claim. The paper concedes this in Table 5's own note and falls back on PSA as the candidate's distinguishing axis. But PSA is reported as N/A for all five baselines *and for both trivial anchors* — the Table 5 PSA column contains exactly one number, 0.5625. So the paper builds careful trivial-anchor discipline for Overclaim Rate (always-sufficient at 0.875, always-insufficient at 0.000) and then abandons that same discipline for the only metric on which its candidate is claimed to win. **No PSA floor is established.** Given the deliberately imbalanced label distribution (7.1 reports verification strength at 32 complete / 24 partial / 8 opaque, and actor identity at 48 complete / 8 conflicting / 8 opaque), a constant-verdict property predictor would score somewhere in the middle of that band, and the reader cannot tell whether 0.5625 clears it. The paper had the anchors, built the machinery, and did not run it on the axis that carries its RQ2 answer.

**(2) Construction-oracle circularity, one degree removed.** Labels come from a rule file authored by the same person who authored the property vocabulary the candidate scorer implements. The reported kappa of 1.0 is not inter-rater agreement — it is a determinism check of one deterministic rule file executed twice, which is guaranteed by construction. The paper is honest about this (T3 states the diagnostic shows the rules execute reproducibly, not that the taxonomy is semantically unambiguous, and commits to revising the benchmark rather than protecting the labels if a human audit disagrees). Honesty does not remove the exposure: PSA measures conformance to one author's rule file, and the scorer, the vocabulary, the oracle and the benchmark object all descend from the same companion preprint.

**(3) The headline 0.75 barely clears the null.** always-sufficient scores 0.875 Overclaim. trace-present and schema-present score 0.750. So the flagship result is that a predicate saying yes on 56 of 64 cases overclaims almost as often as a predicate saying yes on all 64 — on a corpus deliberately built 56/8 in favor of insufficiency. T2 admits the baselines are intentionally weak lower-bound controls, and the claim is scoped as comparative against a documented lower bound. That scoping is defensible in the body, and it is exactly what will be stripped when the number travels; 8.3 anticipates precisely this misuse and calls it weaponized framing.

**(4) One case per cell means the transfer metrics are defined but not measured.** Section 6.2 defines Cross-Regime Transfer and requires regime-aware splits. Section 7.3 then reports that regime and question-family slices are **uniform at 0.5625** and reads them as coverage checks rather than cross-regime generalization. A benchmark whose stated contribution is *cross-regime* sufficiency has, at n=1 per regime-by-degradation cell, no power to measure cross-regime anything. The four reporting views promised in 3.8 (in-regime, leave-one-regime-out, degradation-held-out, complete-versus-degraded) are largely unexercised in the results.

**(5) The eight-surface convergence survey (Table 1) is an uncoded narrative survey.** Section 2.6 concedes it is a non-exhaustive narrative survey of the Q2 2026 landscape, not a systematic search, and preprint- and documentation-heavy. Its cells are graded Yes / Partial / No with no coding protocol, no second coder, and no inclusion criteria — yet the zero-of-N pattern in that table is the stated empirical warrant for benchmarking overclaim at all. Note also that the PDF's Table 1 is visibly mis-typeset (row labels and cell values are misaligned in the text layer), so the exact per-surface gradings are not reliably readable from the rendered artifact.

Minor, but worth carrying: single author, v1 only, arXiv with no venue; no LLM-judge run despite specifying one; no real production traces (F4, gated on IRB review and data-sharing agreements); the scorer's input field list is redacted in the manuscript and disclosed only in the deposit.

## 5. What it FORECLOSES for our paper

Treat all of these as prior art dated 30 May 2026, with a deposited, licensed, mirrored artifact behind them. Any of them asserted as novel in our SoK is a rebuttal handed to a reviewer.

1. **The core presence-is-not-sufficiency framing is published and named.** *Container fallacy* and *Decision Evidence Gap* are taken. Our paper must not present "the record exists, therefore the property is established" as an unnamed or newly observed error. It must be cited on first use of any equivalent formulation.
2. **Overclaim as a quantified diagnostic is taken.** Overclaim Rate, Property Sufficiency Accuracy, Underclaim Rate, Sufficient-claim Rate and the trivial-anchor discipline are all specified and deposited. Our "12 of 14 scorable systems bind lower than advertised" is structurally an overclaim measurement and will be read as one. Better to say so and cite than to have a reviewer say it for us.
3. **A property decomposition of the governance record is taken.** actor / authority / action / policy basis / decision basis / resource touch / lifecycle context / verification strength. We cannot present a decomposition of what a decision record must bind as first-of-kind.
4. **Graded verification strength is taken.** cryptographically bound / schema-validated / replayable / attested / merely narrated. This is the single closest structural neighbor to our **TIER OF ACCEPTANCE** column. Our A-T1/A-T2/A-T3/A-NONE ladder must be positioned as a *different object* — a tier of the mechanism that verifies a claim, applied to systems — and not as the first graded treatment of verification quality in a governance record.
5. **The claim that no published benchmark measures agent-runtime governance evidence is now false.** One does.
6. **The deferral-not-exemption reading of the April 2026 interagency guidance is published.** Section 8.2 states it, correctly, with the scope carve-out and the RFI. Our own standing house reading of that guidance is now co-published prior art; cite it rather than assert it fresh.
7. **The layer separation (evidence plane and governance layer, not orchestration layer) is argued.** Section 2.4 pushes back against Q2-2026 taxonomies that class these methods as planning loops or tool-call routers. Useful to us, but not ours to claim.
8. **The complements-assurance-cases-rather-than-replaces-them positioning is argued** (2.5, against the ICO / Turing Annexe 5 tradition and Reliability Assessment Models). Same treatment.

## 6. What it LEAVES OPEN

Every instrument in our paper survives, and most of them survive *because* of what this paper does not have.

1. **There is no action column. At all.** All eight property families and all eight question families ask what a *record* establishes about a decision that already happened. Not one asks at what tier the *effector* was bounded when the action was taken. Action boundary is the closest, and it is a question about what the record says was requested, allowed, blocked, executed or exceeded — a reconstruction question, not a mediation question. **A record can bind every property of a decision that was never mediated at all**, and DEMM-Bench would score that record sufficient. Our TIER OF ACTION column and the AUTHORIZATION-EXECUTION GAP are unclaimed, and the paper cannot see the failure they name.
2. **Its weakest family is precisely our territory.** Action boundary is the PSA floor at 0.25 and is named the number-one v2 priority (8.4, item 1), with the observation that policy- and lifecycle-derived features do not transfer there and a distinct feature path is required. That is an open invitation, from the incumbent, into the exact cell our contribution occupies.
3. **No effector model, no mediation coverage.** Nothing decomposes file write, subprocess spawn (let alone descendants), network egress, VCS push, package install, credential use, or sub-agent spawn. Nothing scores covered / partial / not-covered per effector. Our seven-effector by fifteen-system coverage grid, and the network-egress result (reached by 14 of 15, bounded by none), have no counterpart here. Section 2.2 dismisses interception and permission-rule literature in a paragraph — as background that fails to *reconstruct*, never as a surface to be *scored*.
4. **No adversary. Anywhere.** Degradation is generator-applied, deterministic, evaluator-independent and logged. missing-delegation, conflicting-identity and partial-graph are maskings, not placements. There is no A1 outside / A2 in inputs / A3 in policy or weights / A4 inside the harness / A5 below the harness. Section 2.4 explicitly states that a sufficient record implies neither legal adequacy nor robust security under prompt injection. F3 lists adversarial fills as *future work*, gated on corpus expansion. Our adversary-placement instrument, primary and secondary, and the finding that all 20 failing composition pairs sit in the A2xA4 cell, are entirely unclaimed — and the A2xA4 result is unreachable by a benchmark with no harness in its threat model.
5. **No enforceability or monitorability axis.** Nothing distinguishes a monitorable safety surrogate from a non-monitorable advertised objective. Our split-enforceability coding has no precedent here.
6. **The object is record formats, not systems.** DEMM-Bench scores eight *regimes* (evidence substrates) across 64 synthetic cases. It never scores a deployed system, never scores a published claim, and names no system as a subject. Our adjudication of fifteen systems' published claims is a different object; T1 (synthetic ground truth) is the paper's own stated largest construct-validity threat, and F4 (real captured traces, gated on IRB review and data-sharing agreements) is unbuilt.
7. **Weakest-link composition within a column is unclaimed.** DEMM-Bench aggregates by strict conjunction across properties *within a case*. It has no notion of two independent columns each carrying its own weakest link, and no notion of a tier lattice.
8. **Supervisory crosswalk — see section 3 above.** Fully open, and pointed at.
9. **Cross-regime generalization is unmeasured** (n=1 per cell). Any claim we make about breadth across systems will not be pre-empted by this paper's breadth claims, because it does not have any that survive its own reporting.

## 7. Discipline this paper imposes on us

Not foreclosure, but a reviewer's checklist we should meet pre-emptively, because this paper will be in the reviewer's hand.

- **Supply a trivial-adjudicator anchor.** They built always-sufficient and always-insufficient anchors and then failed to apply the idea to their winning metric (attack 1 above). We must not repeat the omission. What does a rule that scores every system B-T3 achieve on our instrument? What fraction of the 15 would any conservative reader downgrade without our tier rule?
- **Report genuine second-coder agreement, not self-consistency.** Their kappa of 1.0 is a determinism check dressed as agreement. Our "enforceability is split on 50 of 50 codings" and "A-T2 is empty" are 100-percent and 0-percent results on a self-designed instrument — the exact shape that attracts the same attack. Pre-empt with an independent coder, a stated coding protocol, and preserved disagreement counts.
- **Scope every number to the corpus.** They scope obsessively (on the 64-case deterministic package) and still flag weaponized-framing risk in broader impact. Our fifteen-system numbers need the same treatment.
- **State the lower-bound framing explicitly.** Our adjudication is against *advertised* claims — a documented lower bound on what systems establish, not an absolute bound on what they could establish. Their T2 is the model for how to say this in one paragraph.
- **Say what a passing tier does not establish.** Their 3.1 non-goals block is the cleanest instance of this move in the adjacent literature and is worth matching in structure.

## 8. Suggested related-work paragraph (third person, US English, double-blind safe)

> The closest measurement precedent is DEMM-Bench, which asks whether agent-runtime records across eight evidence regimes are sufficient to reconstruct decision-level properties, and reports that container-presence baselines declare sufficiency on 75 percent of a 64-case synthetic corpus in which a required property is in fact missing [cite]. That work and this one share a premise — the presence of an evidence container is not the property it is taken to establish — but they divide on the object scored: DEMM-Bench scores the reconstructability of a record after a decision, over deterministic, adversary-free degradations of synthetic cases, whereas this paper scores fifteen published systems on two columns at once, the tier at which a claim is verified and the tier at which an action is bounded. That division is load-bearing rather than cosmetic, because a record can bind every property of a decision that was never mediated at all: DEMM-Bench has no effector model, no adversary placement, and no enforcement column, and its own weakest property family is action boundary, at 0.25 property sufficiency accuracy, which it names as its next priority. The authorization-execution gap reported here — seven of fifteen systems verifying acceptance at the strongest tier while one bounds action there — is therefore a failure that a record-sufficiency benchmark is not constructed to see.

Four sentences. Third person throughout, no first-person plural, no system or repository names, no self-identification. Trim to three by dropping the second half of sentence three if space is short.
