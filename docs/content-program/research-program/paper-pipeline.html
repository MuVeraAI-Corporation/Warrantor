Guard Model Research Pipeline

Eight additional papers the completed experiments make reachable — with designs, Modal costs, target venues, and what would kill each one.
Drafted 2026-08-31, after nine pre-registered experiments.

Three papers are in flight. This document covers what the evidence already collected makes newly reachable — each entry is a paper whose central result is either already half-measured or one Modal run away.

Status, 2026-08-31 evening — three of these shipped, and one did not survive contact with the literature. P1 and P2 are written as proposed. P3 was pre-empted: a prior-art check found LongGuard (arXiv 2608.27580, published four days earlier) covering the same phenomenon across 15 guardrails, with the mechanism P3 deferred and the defense P3 only predicted. It is repositioned as a replication note, and a follow-up run (P3-X) tested the one claim we had retracted — confirming the retraction was right. P7 is substantially reduced by that same paper and is rewritten below. Entries are updated in place rather than deleted, so what was proposed and what actually happened stay comparable.
The honest venue note. Most of these are security-measurement papers, whose homes are IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, CCS and SaTML. Three are genuinely NeurIPS-shaped — P2, P3 and P4 — because they contribute a method or a mechanism rather than a measurement. P7 was the Datasets & Benchmarks entry; it no longer is — the dataset it proposed to build now exists at greater scale (see P7). Aiming a measurement paper at the NeurIPS main track costs a cycle for structural reasons, not quality ones.

P1 — The reproducibility floor of guard evaluation

deliveredhighest impactcheapest USENIX Security / S&P

Most published guard comparisons report differences smaller than their own noise floor

Where it came from D1 measured this by accident. Re-scoring the same items at identical temperature and seed on different Modal containers drifted 1.0% (4B) and 5.7% (0.6B) of verdicts. The harness's own note predicts it: llama.cpp is deterministic for a fixed model file, context size and thread count — and nobody pins thread count.
The paper Measure the floor systematically — across serving stacks (llama.cpp, vLLM, transformers), quantizations, thread counts, batch sizes, GPU architectures — then audit the published guard-comparison literature against it. Any claimed improvement below the floor is not a result.
Why it lands It is a wrecking ball with a constructive ending: the deliverable is a reporting protocol and a one-command floor estimator anyone can run before claiming an improvement. Cheap, unarguable, and it makes every subsequent guard paper cite it.
Modal ~8 GPU-hours. Mostly repeated evaluation of a fixed corpus under varied serving configuration.
What would kill it If the floor turns out to be <0.5% under realistic configurations, the audit has no teeth. Mitigate by measuring the floor first and only then deciding whether the audit is worth writing.
Delivered as proposed. “How Reproducible Is a Guard Evaluation?” — eight runs at identical configuration, 998 of 1,000 items identical across all eight, mean pairwise disagreement 0.09%. Two of the three hypotheses were refuted, including the thread-count explanation the serving stack’s own note implicates (agreement 0.9993 within and across thread counts; GPU architecture contributes nothing measurable).

The result cuts against the paper’s own motivation, and that is reported. The 1.0–5.7% drift that motivated this paper came from evaded sets — borderline by construction, since every item was caught then evaded. The stratified floor is 0.09%, so the floor is probably not a single number, and it remains unmeasured precisely where perturbation effects are measured. Folded into T-03 as §5.10.

P2 — Input-variation defenses

deliveredNeurIPS-shapedhas a method NeurIPS / ICML / S&P

The defender should vary the input, not the decoding

Where it came from D1 tested the obvious defense — sample the guard k times and aggregate — and it failed, at 3.4× false-positive cost for 47% recovery. The mechanism is the contribution: the guard is 81% unanimous across 16 draws, so there is nothing to aggregate. The attacker's temperature varies the input; the defender's varied only the decoding. Different mechanisms wearing the same name.
The paper Build the defense that follows from that diagnosis: paraphrase-and-vote. Generate k paraphrases of the incoming text, score each, aggregate. This puts the defender on the axis the model is actually sensitive to. Compare against: the failed decoding-sampling baseline (already measured), a naive threshold shift, and an ensemble of distinct guards.
Why it lands A negative result with a mechanism that predicts a positive one is the strongest shape a defense paper can have. The failed baseline is already run and pre-registered, which makes the comparison credible rather than convenient.
Modal ~25 GPU-hours — paraphrase generation dominates, guard scoring is cheap.
What would kill it If paraphrase-and-vote also lands outside a pre-fixed FPR budget, the paper becomes a second negative result. Fix the budget before running, as D1 did, so that outcome is publishable rather than an embarrassment.
Delivered as proposed. “Vary the Input, Not the Decoding” — the defense that follows from the mechanism, measured rather than argued.

P3 — Why benign framing works

shipped, reframedNeurIPS-shapedmechanism NeurIPS / ACL

A causal account of the strongest evasion family

Where it came from Embedding unsafe content in an ordinary workplace email (T4) beats every other transformation, at 0.51–0.62 candidate-level miss rate across families — roughly double the next best. It was not the strongest under deterministic decoding; its advantage appears under sampling, which suggests variance in where the payload lands.
The paper Decompose the effect into three candidate mechanisms and test them against each other:
  1. Length dilution — hold framing constant, vary filler length.
  2. Positional displacement — hold length constant, vary payload position.
  3. Genuine boundary crossing — measure the guard's verdict-token probability shift, not just the argmax.
The three make different predictions and are separable by construction.
Why it lands It converts the program's largest measured effect into a causal claim, which is what a top ML venue rewards over another measurement.
Modal ~12 GPU-hours.
Runtime caveat, carried from M3. Probability shifts need logits, which means transformers or vLLM — a different serving path from every Ollama-based verdict in this program. Those numbers cannot be compared against existing results without reintroducing the runtime confound M3 was built to eliminate. Report it as a separate analysis with its own baseline.
Pre-empted, and the delivered paper is not the proposed one. The experiment ran and found that position, not length, orders the benign-framing effect — so it shipped as “Position, Not Length,” not as the causal account of framing proposed here. Then a prior-art check found LongGuard, which has every element at greater scale (15 guardrails vs 2; 0.25k–32k vs ~1.2k), plus the attention→logit→behavior mechanism this entry deferred and the chunked-detection defense it only predicted. Repositioned as a workshop replication note. No novelty is claimed.
P3-X — the retraction, tested rather than conceded. Draft 1 read a non-monotone miss rate as refuting length dilution. That was retracted as a probable small-range artifact, and then the range was actually run: lengths {0, 1k, 4k, 16k, 32k} × five positions, 1,260 inputs per model at both scales, zero unparsed output in all 42 cells.

The retraction was right. Over the endpoints both scales are identical and one-directional — 19 payloads of 60 degrade, 0 improve, p < 0.0001 — and the sole non-monotone step is the only step that fails significance (p = 0.688). Dilution was never refuted; it was untested. The one design element LongGuard does not have is our 0× control — the payload with no frame at all — which anchors every cell to an absolute floor instead of a relative baseline. Folded into T-03 as §5.11. Cost: 0.77 GPU-hours, $1.94.

P4 — Disagreement as a usable signal

NeurIPS-shapedNeurIPS / ICLR

Guards agree on benign content and disagree on unsafe content — can that be exploited?

Where it came from M1's cross-family agreement: 95–99% on benign originals, 35–83% on unsafe ones. Two well-configured guards from different publishers disagree on 22% of unsafe items.
The paper Two questions, both testable with data largely in hand:
  1. Is disagreement informative? Does it predict genuine label ambiguity, or is one guard simply right? Requires human adjudication on a disagreement sample — which is also the annotation T-03 already owes.
  2. Is it actionable? A cascade that routes disagreement to a stronger model or to a human. Measure cost against a single-guard baseline at matched FPR.
Why it lands It turns an inconvenient observation into a system design, and the cascade framing is directly deployable.
Modal ~10 GPU-hours plus human annotation (~$300–500 on Prolific), which is the one cost Modal cannot absorb.

P5 — The prevalence trap in safety fine-tuning

S&P / CCS

Concentrating training data on the harms you care about makes the guard worse

Where it came from E1: at equal data volume, reallocating toward a harm cluster cost 8–20 points across seven non-target categories and produced no gain on the targeted ones. Recall fell from 0.9079 to 0.8276 while the false-positive rate fell — the model shifted its operating point toward “safe” rather than sharpening.
The paper The concentration sweep E1 could not afford: 1× / 2× / 4× / 8× target density, three harm clusters, three model scales, both replication splits. Separate reallocation (fixed volume) from addition (more target data) — E1 tested only the first, and they are different products.
Why it lands It contradicts a widely sold practice: vendors offer domain-specialized guards, and this says the reallocation version of that is actively harmful.
Modal ~35 GPU-hours (48 adapters, parallel).

P6 — Guard composition and the independence assumption

shipped, reframedreaches T-12CCS / USENIX

Stacking guards buys much less than defense-in-depth assumes

Where it came from M1: an evasion defeating one guard defeats a guard from a different publisher at 2.5–4× its base rate, and within a family at nearly 6×. Failures are not independent, so a stack's miss rate is nowhere near the product of its members'.
The paper A composition calculus for guard stacks: measure pairwise and higher-order failure correlation across a full panel, derive a corrected estimator for stack FNR, and validate it against measured stacks. Include the selection question — which pairs are most complementary, and does complementarity track family distance?
Why it lands Layered containment is asserted throughout the agent-security literature and, to our reading, never measured. This supplies the number — and it is the same argument T-12 makes about composition, which lets the two papers reinforce each other.
Modal ~20 GPU-hours, largely reusing M1 and M2-X artifacts.
Experiments run 2026-08-31, pre-registered 364800ff. Paper not yet written. Six guards, 1,799 shared items, one configuration. Every one of 15 pairs fails non-independently, intervals excluding 1. Stacking all six: measured FNR 0.1591 against an independence prediction of 0.0031 — an independence penalty of 51×, 95% CI [28.9, 99.7].

The cost side runs the other way, and that is the more interesting half. Stack FPR is 0.50× what independence predicts, because guards also agree on which benign items look unsafe. Correlation destroys the FNR benefit and discounts the FPR cost simultaneously. Absolute cost is still real: 6.7% FPR, 1.54× the worst single member.

H2 was refuted where testable — the penalty is pairwise correlation compounded, not higher-order structure, which makes it estimable from two guards instead of the whole stack. H3 is narrower than proposed: the within-family effect rests on two families, one strongly self-correlated and one not, so family distance is not established as a general selection heuristic.

Cost: $0.15, not the ~20 GPU-hours estimated here. Under OR-composition a stack misses an item only when every member misses it — a boolean function of per-item verdicts already on disk. Only the two-model panel extension needed a GPU.
Pre-empted, and the check caught it before the draft. Layered LLM Defenses as an Ensemble (arXiv 2608.28327, Alotaibi et al., KFUPM/SDAIA) published 2026-08-28, three days before this experiment ran. It states the governing argument, measures failure correlation across a seven-layer stack, and reports all fifteen pairs correlated with the joint residual above the multiplicative prediction, plus false refusals accumulating as a union. That is P6’s H1, H4 and framing. No novelty is claimed on any of them.

The literature check ran BEFORE the first draft this time — the discipline P3 learned the hard way. It cost one tool call and did three things: prevented a false novelty claim, revealed the control we were missing, and running that control produced the result the paper now turns on.
Draft 3 — re-run with their own CMH estimator, and the thesis flipped from contrast to convergence. Reading their §11 in full withdrew three Draft 1 claims (that φ carries no marginal ceiling; that the family groups do not overlap; that our result was the opposite of theirs). Re-running the stratification with their CMH common odds ratio then produced the paper’s strongest result:
  • 8 of 15 pairs survive as they specify it (10 cluster-robust), against their 1 of 15 — their §11.6 is “underpowered by construction” by their own statement.
  • The family split is clean on this statistic, which has no φmax-style ceiling: geometric mean conditional OR 32.24 within-family vs 1.75 cross-family, 4/4 vs 4/11 surviving.
  • Their one survivor and our strongest agree. Two probes sharing “an input representation and a training pipeline”: OR 158.7. Two scales of one guard family: OR 158.00. Their same-row pair without shared training failed (1.39); so do our cross-family pairs (1.75). Both studies: shared lineage survives conditioning, shared category does not.
  • One genuinely complementary pair — OR 0.58, CI [0.38, 0.96], excluding 1 from below.
  • ⚠️ An asymmetry recorded rather than smoothed: conditioning strengthened their pair (68→159) and weakened all four of ours (789→158). Difficulty explains part of ours and none of theirs.
Unchanged: all 15 pairs correlated; 51× penalty [28.9, 99.7]; 15.9% missed where independence predicts 0.31%; FPR penalty 0.50× but 1.54× the worst member.

Draft 3: P6-composition-independence-paper.md, 6,513 words, retitled “Shared Lineage, Not Shared Category, Makes Guards Fail Together” to lead with the convergence. Deliberately not titled on the 158.7-vs-158.00 match, which the paper itself calls a coincidence. Total cost $0.15 — every analysis after the panel extension was a re-analysis of verdicts on disk.

P7 — The long-context guard benchmark

largely pre-emptedbuilds an assetworkshop, not D&B

The benchmark suite the field uses cannot exhibit the failure mode it worries about

The premise held; the paper did not. The motivating negative result is real and unchanged — E3 found zero decision changes across a sixteenfold context-window range, because no corpus item reaches even 2048 tokens (median 528, p99 1706). But the benchmark this entry proposed to build has largely been built. LongGuard (arXiv 2608.27580, 2026-08-27) constructs a 0.25k–32k grid with position varied independently of length, evaluates 15 guardrails on it, traces an attention→logit→behavior mechanism, and ships two training-free mitigations. A D&B submission whose dataset already exists at greater scale is not a D&B submission.
What we ran instead, and what it settled. P3-X replicated on two models LongGuard did not test — 1,260 inputs per model across the same length and position grid, zero unparsed output in all 42 cells. 19 of 60 payloads degrade and 0 improve from 1k to 32k, identically at both scales (p < 0.0001). Two things came out of it that are worth carrying forward:
  • The 0× control is a real design contribution, and a small one. A needle-in-haystack grid always has a haystack, so its cells are read against a relative baseline. Ours includes the payload alone — 0/60 missed unframed at the 4B — which anchors every cell to an absolute floor. That is a paragraph in someone’s methods section, not a paper.
  • Scale does not help here, and that is the unusual part. Across four other perturbations the 0.6B is reliably less stable than the 4B. Under long-input dilution the payload-level degradation is identical at both. This is the one hazard we measure where reaching for a bigger guard buys nothing.
What is actually left Narrow, and worth stating as narrow. (a) Beyond 32k — LongGuard’s grid stops there; the 128k regime that real agent transcripts and retrieval contexts reach is untested by anyone we have read. (b) Naturalistic filler — ours is one benign corpus reused by design, to hold content constant; whether the effect survives filler that is topically adjacent to the payload is a different and more deployment-relevant question. (c) Multi-turn and retrieval-stuffed variants.
Check before building — we have not read LongGuard in full. Our knowledge of it comes from a prior-art comparison, not a full-text read note like the ones backing our other citations. Whether (c) is already covered by them is unverified, and this program has now been bitten twice by treating a status marker as evidence of reading. Read the paper before spending a GPU-hour on any of the above.
Modal The original ~15 GPU-hour estimate is void. Scoped to (a) alone, at P3-X’s measured throughput and 4× the context, roughly 3–5 GPU-hours — but only after the read above.

P8 — Quantization-aware safety evaluation

S&P / MLSys

Every guard benchmark that omits its serving precision is under-specified

Where it came from M3 and M3-X: at Q4_K_M — the deployed default — 4 to 18 verdicts per thousand differ from each model's own f16 reference, in every one of five models across three families. The direction is model-specific: one model is 84-to-0 safety-adverse at Q2_K, another runs usability-adverse then reverses, a third is symmetric.
The paper Extend to the full panel and to quantization schemes beyond GGUF K-quants (AWQ, GPTQ, bitsandbytes NF4), and pair it with P1's noise floor so that verdict movement is separated from serving noise — which the current M3 numbers, at 4–11 per thousand, cannot fully do.
Why it lands It hands the field a protocol, and safety-critical deployment on quantized weights is universal and undocumented.
Modal ~20 GPU-hours.
Dependency. This paper is materially stronger after P1. Several M3 effects sit near the reproducibility floor, and saying so honestly requires having measured the floor first.
Two-thirds cut before a single GPU-second, by a check run before the DESIGN. Quality Is Not a Safety Proxy Under Quantization (arXiv 2606.10154) already ran the cross-scheme safety audit — 51 rows, GGUF + AWQ + GPTQ. Certifying Compressed Language Models (arXiv 2608.15046) already built the equivalence toolkit, on an atlas of 1,707 paired cells where churn runs ~5× the net delta.

The scheme extension was cut, and not for cost: [Certify] show the GPTQ-vs-AWQ ordering reverses with the calibration draw alone in 5 of 8 cells, so running it uncontrolled would have produced an artifact. Declining to run it is what the check bought.
What remained — guards, which neither paper measures. Their objects are generative refusal via an LLM judge, and benchmark accuracy. A guard’s verdict is the measurement.
  • The ~5× ratio does not transfer. Guards run 1.4× — and we tested whether our arm-separated denominator explained it. It does not: the pooled, directly comparable ratio is also 1.4×.
  • Direction is a MODEL property, not a bit-width property. ShieldGemma 9B is 0.97 safety-adverse (144 unsafe→safe vs 4 the other way); Qwen3Guard 4B runs usability-adverse (0.33). ShieldGemma 2B reverses mid-ladder. This is the decomposition their symmetric accuracy churn cannot have.
  • Three of five models cannot support a ±1pp equivalence claim at n=1,000.
  • ⚠️ We raised a concern that would have killed the result, then measured it away. Against T-03’s borderline floor, 27 of 30 cells would have been indistinguishable from serving noise. So we measured the floor on this corpus: 0.107%, 2 unstable items of 1,000 — the stratified figure, not the borderline one. The concern was wrong and the registered conclusion stands.
Draft 2: P8-quantization-equivalence-paper.md, 4,697 words, “One Ladder, Opposite Directions”. Cost $0.56.

Finishing the [Certify] read corrected the draft three times and produced its best result. Corrections, all against us: we compared a cellwise median to their ratio-of-medians (real figures 1.86× vs 3.85×, not 1.4 vs 5.4); we reported required sample sizes without running the test they size; and we had not fixed the population their atlas licenses.

The addition: their gray zone is empty for guards. [Certify]’s empirical core is that the modal compressed-model cell can be neither certified nor shown different — 69.2% of their S1 and 77.7% of S2. Running TOST on all 30 guard cells: 70.0% certifiable, 30.0% detectable, 0.0% gray zone. Guards churn little enough that 1,000 items settles it. Certifying a quantized guard is cheap; certifying a quantized general model is not. Remaining read debt: [QualityProxy] is still a summary, not full text.

The constraint that is not compute. P4 needs human adjudication, and T-03 owed an unperformed semantic-equivalence annotation. The T-03 half is now a bounded task rather than an open one: a blinded 40-item pack is built and verified — 24 evaded items, 12 caught, 4 attention checks, stratified across all five transformations, with the analyzer written before the pack was generated. It is roughly 35 minutes for one annotator, not $300–500.

Why it cannot be automated away. Our transformation QC proves the rephrasings differ from their originals; the claim needs them to mean the same thing, which is the opposite direction. Cosine similarity cannot settle it — at T4’s 5.93× median length ratio, similarity falls mechanically because text was appended. And an LLM judge would be circular in a paper about language models misjudging text. P4 still needs its own annotation budget.
What actually makes these citable. Not the experiment count. Nine pre-registered, hashed experiments with published per-item outputs — including two withdrawn findings and a defense that failed — is rare in this literature. The artifact is what makes a paper the reference others measure against, and it is already mostly built.
