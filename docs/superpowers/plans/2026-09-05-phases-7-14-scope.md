# Phases 7–14 — scope for the 132 blueprint items the implementation plan does not cover

**Status: scope and decomposition only.** No task here is in step form, and none is in structural
form yet either. This document does the judgment half — which items belong together, what each
group's failure mode is, what it must not become, and what has to be true before it starts. The
seeding and expansion halves are lane work, specified in
`docs/agent-prompts/wave-3-glm-phase-seeding.md` and
`docs/agent-prompts/wave-3-minimax-operator-estate.md`.

## Why this document exists

`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-02-native-ai-platform-os-implementation.md` carries 41 tasks and
341 steps. Measured on 2026-09-05, those tasks reference **57** of the master blueprint's **189**
catalog items. Finishing the entire plan therefore delivers about 30% of the platform the blueprint
specifies. This document scopes the other **132**.

Counted from `docs/html/warrantor-native-ai-platform-os-master-2026-09-01.html`, the uncovered set
is 118 `status=none` and 14 `status=partial`; 60 `core`, 52 `novel`, 9 `clone`, 8 `compose`,
2 `align`, 1 `consume`; and by the blueprint's own waves, 3 W1, 80 W2, 46 W3, 3 W4.

**The three W1 items are the finding.** W1 is the wave everything else was supposed to sit on, and
`L2-05`, `L3-04` and `L4-25` were left out of the plan. They lead Phases 8 and 7 accordingly.

## The rule this document is written under

Every phase below adds surface area, and surface area is what the existing plan is trying to make
provable. So the ordering constraint is not "what is most valuable" but **"what can be claimed
honestly once it exists."** A new adapter that is not mediated is a new orphan, and the wiring
census on `feat/wiring-census` already reports 9 of 38 crates reachable — 29 compile and are called
by nothing. Nothing in Phases 7–14 may raise the orphan count. Each task's exit gate must lower it
or hold it, and the census is the gate that says so.

## Dependency spine

```
  Phase 7  Effect surface breadth          needs Phase 2 (effect decomposition registry)
  Phase 8  Runtime governors and budgets   needs Phase 2, Phase 3
  Phase 9  Authority algebra and channels  needs Phase 1 (delegation algebra), Phase 3
  Phase 10 Evidence depth and custody      needs Phase 1, Phase 5
  Phase 11 The operator estate             needs Phase 4  ·  parallel to 7-10, fixtures-first
  Phase 12 The learning flywheel           needs Phase 10 (receipts are its raw material)
  Phase 13 Assurance and agent lifecycle   needs Phase 8, Phase 10
  Phase 14 Ecosystem and sovereignty       needs Phase 9, Phase 10, Phase 13
```

Phase 11 is deliberately off the spine, the same way Phase 4 is: it builds against frozen contract
fixtures, so the operator lane runs parallel to the Rust lanes rather than queueing behind them.

**76 tasks across 8 phases.** Phases 0–6 average 5.9 tasks per task-phase and 8.3 steps per task;
at that rate Phases 7–14 add roughly 630 steps, which would take the whole plan to about 117 tasks
and 970 steps.

## Phase 7 — Effect surface breadth (16 items, 8 tasks)

**Needs Phase 2.** Every adapter here is a client of the effect-decomposition registry. Cloning
adapters before the registry exists produces nine separately-invented decompositions, which is the
defect Task 2.1 is routed to Opus to prevent.

**The phase's failure mode:** breadth read as capability. Nine `clone` items make this the cheapest
phase to produce and the easiest to overclaim — an adapter that reaches a system is not an adapter
that mediates it.

| Task | Items | Anchor — the failure mode it exists to prevent | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7.1** Effect-class SDK, conformance kit, and the non-amendable deny floor | L4-20, L4-25 | Build the kit before the clones so the nine adapters are transcription rather than nine designs. L4-25 is the floor no agent-amendable policy may reach: metadata and link-local addresses are denied outside the policy engine, so a policy bug cannot open them | `opus` |
| **7.2** Deserialization and template-injection guard | L4-06 | The parser is an effect surface. A guard that inspects the request but not what deserializing it executes is decoration | `glm-5.3-flash` |
| **7.3** Adapters: messaging, ticketing, email | L4-10, L4-11, L4-12 | Three MSG-class surfaces. Email is the one that leaves the organization, so it is the one whose bound must be strongest, not the one that gets a shared implementation | `glm-5.3-flash` |
| **7.4** Adapters: cloud control plane, CI/CD, identity providers | L4-13, L4-14, L4-26 | Three MUTATE-class surfaces that can rewrite the substrate governing them. Each needs the L4-25 floor and Task 3.7's self-protection generalization, or the agent governs its own governor | `opus` |
| **7.5** Adapters: package registries, data platforms, payment rails | L4-15, L4-16, L4-17 | Payment rails are SPEND-class and irreversible. Do not let the shared adapter shape flatten reversibility into a boolean | `glm-5.3-flash` |
| **7.6** Adapter: browser and computer use | L4-18 | The hardest one, and `status=partial`. A computer-use surface defeats the syscall model by construction: every effect arrives as a pixel click. Decompose or state plainly that it is unmediated | `opus` |
| **7.7** Semantic egress purpose and data-class binding, with third-party jurisdiction | L4-24, L4-19 | Destination allow-lists answer *where*. Neither answers *what for* or *what data*, which is invariant I-03, reported unimplemented by the Task 5.1 corpus with no plan task fixing it. This is that task | `opus` |
| **7.8** Recovery orchestration and integrity verification | L4-21 | Recovery is an effect class. An untracked restore is an unlogged mutation with root authority | `glm-5.3-flash` |

**Needs Phases 2 and 3.** Ten of fifteen items are `novel`, so this is the most design-dense phase

its enforcement tier and point at the line that refuses.

| Task | Items | Anchor | Route |
|---|---|---|---|

| **8.2** The risk dial and the end-of-budget risk profile | L3-02, L3-07 | Graduated tightening is only real if loosening is receipted too. And an agent near the end of its budget behaves differently — that profile is a control input, not a metric | `opus` |
| **8.3** Parallelism governor; backpressure, rate and duplicate-effect control | L3-05, L3-10 | The incident's tenth hop was volume. A per-action bound that ignores fan-out bounds nothing | `glm-5.3-flash` |
| **8.4** Population budget ledger, budget transfer, compute-provenance accounting | L3-11, L3-08 | Per-agent budgets sum to no bound at all when agents can transfer. The ledger is the population-level bound and the transfer is the control point | `glm-5.3-flash` |
| **8.5** Task-validity attestation and objective-drift monitor | L3-01, L3-09 | An agent completing a task it was never validly given is the failure both of these catch, one before and one during | `opus` |
| **8.6** Self-risking-experiment detection | L3-06 | An agent testing its own containment is the highest-signal event this system can observe, and today it is indistinguishable from work | `opus` |
| **8.7** Supporting-service hardening standard and container-reset semantics | L2-05, L2-07 | **L2-05 is W1 and was omitted.** The supporting services are inside the trust boundary and hardened by nothing. Reset is a governed operation because it destroys evidence | `glm-5.3-flash` |

**Each of those tasks must convert a specific `#[ignore]` in `rust/warrant/tests/invariants/` into
a passing test and raise `tools/ci/invariant-ratchet.json`'s floor in the same commit.** That is
the cleanest exit gate available anywhere in this document: the corpus already states the failure.

| Task | Items | Anchor | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **9.1** Bounded revocation with a stated SLA | L6-04 | Invariant I-05. Corpus says: no revocation source exists for the identity half, and credential-vault is orphaned so the hop has no boundary | `opus` |
| **9.2** Quorum authority for high-consequence classes | L6-05 | `partial`. Two-person control that one compromised operator can satisfy twice is one-person control with extra latency | `glm-5.3-flash` |
| **9.3** Purpose binding on authority | L6-07 | Invariant I-03, reported unimplemented four ways: no purpose field on the request, none on bounds, none on receipts, none in the WAR predicate. Pairs with Task 7.7 | `opus` |
| **9.4** Authority-expansion detection and sibling-collusion intersection | L6-09, L6-12 | Invariant I-02. Two siblings each within bounds can compose a capability neither holds; intersection over the chain does not catch it because they are not in one chain | `opus` |

| **9.6** Attested sender identity and the channel policy language | L5-06, L5-05 | Unauthenticated agent-to-agent messaging is the substrate for every collusion control above it. Do this before 9.7 | `glm-5.3-flash` |

| **9.8** Escalation channel to humans, reachable and attractive | L5-04 | An escalation path that is slower than proceeding is not a path. Measure its use rate, and treat zero as a defect | `glm-5.3-flash` |

## Phase 11 — The operator estate (24 items, 13 tasks)

**Needs Phase 4; runs parallel to 7–10 against fixtures.** All 13 tasks route to `minimax-m3`.
This is the largest phase in the document and the one the blueprint's own status data flags hardest:
24 of 28 L8 items are uncovered, and L8 is the plane a buyer actually touches.

**The rule that overrides every design instinct here is unchanged and non-negotiable:** no screen,
badge, summary, export or status may collapse observed, advisory, mediated and enforced into one
green state, and every rendered guarantee must also render what it does not cover.

| Task | Items | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **11.1** Fleet console and the agent inventory register | L8-02, L8-08 | `partial`. An inventory that lists agents but not their authority is a nameplate, not a register |
| **11.2** Incident console; containment latency and exercised blast-radius bounds | L8-03, L8-25 | A blast-radius bound that has never been exercised is an estimate. Render the distribution, not the claim |
| **11.3** Policy studio for non-developers | L8-04 | The compiler already refuses; the studio must refuse in the same words, or the two disagree and the studio wins in the user's head |
| **11.4** Audit workbench and evidence pack generator | L8-05, L8-10 | The pack must verify offline, on a machine that never touched this deployment |
| **11.5** Plain-language scope and dependency-aware partial approval | L8-14, L8-15 | Releasing part of a batch must not silently release dependent effects. Wave 1's Task 4.1 built the queue; this is its consequence model |
| **11.6** Approval quality, cognitive load, automation bias, oversight yield | L8-17, L8-23 | A disagreement rate trending to zero while latency improves is rubber-stamping. Instrument it or the queue becomes ceremony |
| **11.7** Accessibility as a control property; onboarding and first run | L8-18, L8-12 | `partial`. An access barrier inside a safety-critical workflow is a control failure, not a polish item |

| **11.9** Supervisor/regulator portal and the customer trust center | L8-06, L8-28 | Both are read-only views of the same receipts. If they can disagree, one of them is a marketing surface |
| **11.10** Executive risk view; exposure budget and risk-appetite ledger | L8-07, L8-21 | Risk appetite that is not a number the system enforces is a slide |
| **11.11** Outcome contract, baseline, attestation; safe-autonomy expansion ledger | L8-20, L8-24 | Autonomy should widen on evidence and narrow on incident, and both directions must be receipted |
| **11.12** Notice, rights, appeal, contestability; role competency assurance | L8-19, L8-16 | The affected party is not the operator. This is the only surface in the estate built for someone outside the buying organization |
