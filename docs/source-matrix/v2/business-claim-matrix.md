# Business, market, procurement, and regional claim matrix

Status: multi-wave evidence reconciliation; customer and tender validation pending  
Snapshot: 2026-09-01  
Structured evidence: 201 candidate sources; 117 promoted sources; 29 repository claims

This matrix separates market attractiveness from evidence that can support a forecast. Public adoption surveys can show direction, official product terms can bound packaging and price anchors, and procurement rules can define buyer constraints. None of those, alone or together, proves Warrantor's obtainable revenue. Revenue, margin, conversion, and sales-cycle claims therefore remain hypotheses until they are tied to a named customer segment, purchasing unit, deployment pattern, measured cost base, and observed willingness to pay.

## Packaging and pricing architecture

The recommended structure deliberately separates adoption, assurance and high-touch deployment. Exact prices remain experiments, not conclusions.

| Offer | Intended user and outcome | Proposed meter | Open/paid boundary | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warrantor Community | Engineers can create, verify and inspect portable warrants, receipts and conformance vectors locally | Free | Schemas, SDKs, CLI/verifier, reference policy mappings, test vectors and baseline conformance remain open | A weak open core will not build trust or ecosystem adoption |
| Warrantor Integrations | Platform teams connect MCP, gateways, AuthZEN, Cedar, OPA, identity and evidence systems | Connector support tier or included enterprise entitlement | Common connectors should be open where interoperability benefits outweigh support burden | Charging for every connector creates integration resistance and fork incentives |
| Warrantor Assurance Cloud | Security, risk and audit teams verify actions, retain evidence, query exceptions and export assurance packs | Base platform subscription plus verified bundles/events and retention | Local verification remains open; managed verification, retention, analytics and service guarantees are paid | Usage pricing becomes unpredictable without caps and observable value units |
| Warrantor Enterprise | Regulated enterprises need SSO, private networking, policy administration, HA, support, audit exports and conformance management | Annual platform subscription, scoped by deployment/environment and service level | Enterprise administration and operations are paid, not the protocol | Long sales cycles, procurement friction and incumbent bundling |
| Warrantor Sovereign | Government and regulated buyers need regional operation, local partner delivery, residency and custom assurance mappings | Annual committed contract plus implementation/support | Country profiles can be open; validated operation, local support and bespoke assurance are paid | Lower margin, partner dependency and country-specific legal obligations |

### Metering recommendations

1. Use **verified evidence bundles** as the primary usage unit when buyers value assurance, with clear caps, retention tiers and failed-verification visibility.
2. Use **mediated actions** only where Warrantor is demonstrably on the enforcement path. Never charge twice for authorization and its receipt without a buyer-visible distinction.
3. Treat model/GPU compute as **pass-through or separately disclosed managed cost**. Warrantor should not depend on inference margin unless it intentionally becomes a model host.
4. Do not use per-seat pricing as the only unit for machine-scale agent activity. Seats can price administrative and analyst access while machine events price service consumption.
5. Offer committed-use discounts only after workload variance, storage amplification and peak verifier capacity are measured.

### Unit-economics model required before a margin claim

For each workload profile, record:

- authorization and evidence-verification CPU time;
- cryptographic operations, external timestamp/log calls and confidential-computing premium;
- bytes stored per action, index amplification, retention and export traffic;
- failed/retried action rate and asynchronous reconciliation cost;
- tenant isolation, key management, observability and disaster-recovery overhead;
- support and security-response hours by customer tier;
- partner, marketplace, payment and cloud-commit discounts;
- professional-services content separated from recurring software revenue.

Report gross margin by offer and scenario. A blended figure is misleading until customer mix is known.

## Bottom-up market model

The model should be a transparent calculation, not a sourced-looking top-down number:

`annual revenue = eligible organizations × reachable share × qualified-opportunity rate × win rate × deployed-environment ACV × in-year revenue factor`

Build separate rows for regulated financial services, government/sovereign AI, enterprise agent platforms and AI infrastructure vendors. For each row disclose:

| Input | Acceptable evidence | Disqualifying shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible organizations | Named regulator lists, procurement systems, or reproducible company datasets | Applying a global AI-software market percentage |
| Reachable share | Supported geographies, integrations, partner coverage and sales capacity | Assuming every organization is reachable in year one |
| Qualified-opportunity rate | Discovery interviews or comparable funnel data | Equating reported AI use with buying Warrantor |
| Win rate | Observed pilots/proposals or a labeled scenario assumption | Borrowing a generic SaaS conversion rate without context |
| ACV | Willingness-to-pay tests, comparable procurement awards and scoped deployment economics | Treating non-comparable user or request list prices as Warrantor ACV |
| In-year factor | Procurement, pilot, security-review and implementation timing | Counting full annual value for every late-year close |

The base case should remain a hypothesis until real funnel evidence exists. Publish low/base/high ranges and a sensitivity chart; never compress uncertainty into one headline.

## Regional entry profiles

### Qatar: regulated-finance assurance wedge

- Start with QCB-regulated organizations because the AI Guideline supplies concrete provider, contracting, supplier-assessment, high-risk substitutability and approval fields.
- Generate an evidence register that maps every field to a source artifact, responsible owner, freshness rule and verifier result.

- Do not generalize QCB requirements to every Qatari buyer or claim that an open-source component must itself form a local entity.
- Gate: two regulated design partners, QCB-specialist legal review, provider/subprocessor inventory, high-risk approval workflow and tested exit/substitutability plan.

### UAE: procurement and evidence profile

- Align the offer to digital procurement, data/residency, risk and audit evidence instead of generic national AI ambition.
- The current official English overview links to detailed material that is Arabic-only; it does not satisfy this library's English full-text rule for detailed control extraction.
- Obtain an authoritative English version or another English primary source before encoding detailed procurement controls.
- Gate: federal/emirate scope separation, tender sample, data-location mapping, local partner economics and procurement-authority validation.

### Saudi Arabia: digital-control and sovereign operations profile

- Map Warrantor outputs to DGA digital-transformation and cloud-governance evidence, then test sector overlays rather than declaring a universal national profile.
- Separate national strategy sources from mandatory technical controls and tender requirements.
- Gate: official control crosswalk, sector applicability, cloud/hosting route, Arabic-source translation policy for delivery teams, and at least two buyer or auditor validations.

### India: ecosystem, compute and public-sector route

- Use IndiaAI compute pricing and end-user policy as infrastructure and operating constraints, not evidence of demand for Warrantor.
- Investigate empanelment, marketplace, public-procurement, sector-regulator and local-support requirements before forecasting revenue.
- Make local verifier, cross-language SDKs and open standards the adoption wedge; add paid managed assurance only after operational fit is demonstrated.
- Gate: empanelment eligibility, procurement route, data-location and incident obligations, two ecosystem partners, cost model under subsidized and commercial compute.

## Buyer discovery and procurement questions

For every interview, record buyer role, organization class, geography, current stack, evidence sensitivity and confidence. Do not pool materially different segments.

1. Which event makes the problem urgent: security incident, audit finding, regulatory approval, procurement requirement, insurer request or platform rollout?
2. Who owns the budget, who can veto, and which existing budget category would fund it?
3. What evidence is created today, who trusts it, and which missing proof blocks deployment or approval?
4. Which controls must be inline, which can be detective, and what latency/availability budget can enforcement consume?
5. Which identity, policy, gateway, observability, SIEM, ticketing and audit systems must remain authoritative?
6. What deployment boundary is acceptable: SaaS, private SaaS, customer cloud, on-premises or sovereign operator?
7. What data may leave the environment, what must be selectively disclosed, and how long must evidence be retained?
8. What happens if Warrantor is unavailable, degraded, compromised or produces a false denial?
9. Which procurement, legal, security-review, model-risk and change-approval steps determine time to production?
10. What would the buyer pay to remove the blocker, and what incumbent bundle or internal build is the alternative?

## Decision rule

Until these gates are met, the recommended commercial status is **proceed with segment discovery and technical design partners; defer externally asserted market size, margin, compliance completeness and first-year revenue claims**. This is not a recommendation to defer product development. It is a recommendation to make development milestones generate the evidence needed for credible procurement, pricing and financing decisions.
