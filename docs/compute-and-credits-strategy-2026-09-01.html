1. The inversion: spending is the qualification

The single most important thing in this document

The $5,000 Azure credit did not originate with Azure. It came through NVIDIA Inception's Benefit Explorer, which places you at Milestone 2 of the Microsoft for Startups Founders Hub ladder. The rungs above it are published, and they are earned by usage:

MilestoneCreditRequirement
M2 ← you are here$5,000Business verification
M3$25,0005+ Azure workloads sustained over ~60 days
M4$50,0007+ Azure workloads sustained over ~60 days
M5$150,00010+ workloads AND ~$3,000/month sustained over ~60 days
Why this changes how to spend

Progression is automatic — no application, no pitch, no demo video, and no institutional funding requirement. Bootstrapped companies qualify on technical usage metrics alone.

So the instinct to husband $5,000 is precisely wrong. A frugal month leaves you at $5,000 indefinitely. A month with ten live workloads and $3,000 of genuine usage converts the balance into $150,000 — a thirtyfold return on spending money you were given.

The question is not “how do we make $5,000 last?” It is “what ten workloads do we genuinely need, and can we honestly run $3,000/month through them?”

A correction I owe you, recorded rather than buried

Earlier today I recommended deleting the AumOS API Management service on the evidence that it was ten hours old, fronted the Foundry endpoint, and had served zero requests in its entire lifetime while billing roughly $150/month. On zero-traffic grounds that was right, and it has been deleted — with its config snapshotted to docs/infra/apim-aumos-snapshot-2026-09-01.json, so it is re-creatable.

On ladder grounds it was wrong. APIM is a workload, and $150/month is 5% of the M5 threshold. The right move is not to leave a gateway running by accident — it is to re-create it deliberately as one named component of the portfolio in §5, with real traffic routed through it. An idle gateway is waste; a gateway enforcing per-consumer token budgets is both a genuine workload and a live demonstration of what Warrantor argues for.

A correction to this document, and it is mine

An earlier version of §2 and §8 stated that every GPU family was at limit 0 on both lanes, and that the July note in CLAUDE.md recording 8×A100 / 8×H100 / 8×MI300 was wrong. The July note was right and I was wrong.

The Foundry portal shows A100_80GB 8, H100_80GB 8, MI300_192GB 8, H200_141GB 8 — total quota, zero used, all available. The H200 line is new since July.

What I got wrong: I queried Microsoft.MachineLearningServices/locations/*/quotas and Microsoft.Compute/locations/*/usages. Those report the AML workspace and raw VM quota surfaces. Foundry managed compute is a third surface, and it does not appear in either. Reading two APIs and concluding “every lane is closed” was an over-generalisation from an incomplete sweep — the same defect class this repository documents, committed inside a document that warns about it.

What remains true: raw GPU VMs really are at 0, and lowPriorityCores really is 3. So the VM lane is shut and the portal quota request still has value — but Azure GPU training is available today through Foundry managed compute, and nothing was blocking it.

The licence boundary that matters for Warrantor specifically

NVIDIA Build's free tier is scoped to prototyping, research and evaluation. Serving real end users or business transactions requires an NVIDIA AI Enterprise licence.

The document says this must be closed before submission. It is one paper read. Everything else here is worth less until a submittable paper is actually submittable.

  2. Stand up the three-lineage judging panel on GlobalBatch

    This is the infrastructure every later experiment reuses, it is the cheapest thing Foundry does, and it implements the standing doctrine that open weights teach while frontier models only judge. Three lineages give it the independence your own research showed matters.

  3. Re-measure every published figure at scale

    The current guard figures rest on a 14-case bench and a local Ollama. Batch inference lets you run the full corpora with intervals that are actually narrow. Several claims would move from “consistent with” to “measured”, and any that fail are far better found now than by a reviewer.

  4. Then, and only then, spend GPU time

    Three of eight guard models need GPU. Modal fits all of them and $236 of the grant remains. Do not authorise Modal spend before the Hugging Face token exists — every recipe fails at the data step with HTTP 401 first, which the earlier brief established and nothing has changed.

8. Risks I would not paper over

  • Two ARM quota APIs are not the whole picture — I learned this by being wrong in this document. Microsoft.Compute/*/usages and MachineLearningServices/*/quotas both report 0 for every GPU family, and both are accurate about the surfaces they cover. Foundry managed compute is a third surface they do not describe, and it has 8 each of A100_80GB, H100_80GB, MI300_192GB and H200_141GB, entirely unused. Check the Foundry portal directly before concluding a lane is closed.
  • Milestone thresholds are reported, not contractual. The $25k/$50k/$150k figures come from Microsoft support answers, not a published SLA. Treat them as a strong plan rather than a guarantee, and re-check before betting on a specific number.
  • DGX Cloud credits are “up to”. Awards vary by startup profile. Do not design a training programme around $100,000 before it is granted.
  • OpenRouter free routes are not reproducible. Free endpoints change model versions and providers without notice. Fine for exploration, unusable for a figure you intend to publish.
  • Sustained $3,000/month is real money if credits lapse. The ladder only works while credits cover the spend. Check the expiry on the current grant before committing to a burn rate that assumes it.
  • Nothing here has been verified in your browser sessions. The Inception, OpenRouter, Hugging Face and NVIDIA Build figures are from public documentation, not from your accounts. What your membership actually grants can only be read from the portal.
