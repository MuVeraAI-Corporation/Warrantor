01 · The reframe

Microsoft shipped your product in April, and that changes the question

Verified independently, 2026-08-09

microsoft/agent-governance-toolkit &mdash; MIT-licensed, open source, created 2026-03-02, 5,840 stars, 1,008 forks, last push yesterday. Its own description: &ldquo;Policy enforcement, zero-trust identity, execution sandboxing, and reliability engineering for autonomous AI agents.&rdquo; Seven packages across Python, TypeScript, Rust, Go and .NET. Claims &lt;0.1&nbsp;ms p99 policy enforcement and 10/10 OWASP Agentic Top 10 coverage. Ships SECURITY.md with a 24h/72h/90-day SLA, GOVERNANCE.md, MAINTAINERS.md, CodeQL, Gitleaks, ClusterFuzzLite, OpenSSF Scorecard and 992 conformance tests. [EXTERNAL, verified via GitHub API]

This inverts the objection you were expecting. The normal OSS problem is entity risk &mdash; who maintains this in eighteen months. A buyer can now get an open agent-governance substrate with Microsoft behind it, so AumOS carries the entity risk and has to explain the overlap. Every partner conversation from now on opens with &ldquo;why not the Microsoft one?&rdquo;

And the answer already exists in your own audit

Microsoft's toolkit is a policy and identity runtime. It enforces decisions. It does not produce signed, independently verifiable evidence &mdash; an artifact a third party can check without trusting the runner. That is precisely the slot the competitive sweep found unoccupied by anyone: promptfoo, Inspect, HELM and lm-eval-harness all market reproducibility and none of them signs anything or pins the grader.

So the defensible line is not &ldquo;we govern agents better.&rdquo; It is: enforcement is commodity; verifiable evidence is not. Let Microsoft, AWS Cedar and Envoy do enforcement. Be the layer that makes what happened provable to someone who was not there &mdash; and portable across clouds, which a US-hosted control plane structurally cannot be for an Indian or Gulf regulator.

And the one everybody wastes months on

Do not chase SOC 2 Type II. It attests a service organization. A self-hosted OSS component has no service to attest, and the tooling is built for cloud-hosted companies. Saying that confidently &mdash; then handing over signed releases, an SBOM, a disclosure policy and a completed CSA AI-CAIQ &mdash; is a stronger answer than a certification that does not fit. The pilot bar, and the base rates you are fighting

  • IDC/Lenovo: 88% of AI agent PoCs never reach broad production &mdash; for every 33 pilots, four go live. Attributed to organisational readiness in data, process and infrastructure, not model quality. [EXTERNAL]
  • Gartner: over 40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end-2027 &mdash; escalating cost, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls. Note what is not on that list: technical capability. [EXTERNAL]
  • Phase 1 must be observe-only. For an authority and containment substrate, the difference between a pilot and an outage is a mode that emits decisions without blocking. One false-positive block in a business-critical flow ends the pilot.
  • Latency is the make-or-break number. Microsoft claims &lt;0.1&nbsp;ms p99 for the same interception point. AumOS has eleven published latency budgets and zero benchmarks. Silence is read as slow.
  • Sign-off is three signatures: the engineering sponsor, the security architecture review board, and &mdash; in a regulated firm &mdash; model risk or internal audit. Legal/procurement runs in parallel.
  • The expensive failure mode is the security review starting after the technical pilot succeeds, and a missing SBOM or unsigned release killing a deal that had already won on merit.

The asset you already own and have not claimed

What separates credible multi-language projects from ones that quietly lose trust is a public, dated, executable conformance suite. Sigstore publishes theirs daily &mdash; cosign, sigstore-js and sigstore-rust at 100%, python 96%, java 95% &mdash; which is why sigstore-go can call itself production-ready at 90 stars. AumOS's conformance runner is already at 220/220 across four languages and is genuinely fail-closed &mdash; removing a verifier yields FAIL, never a skip. That is the parity claim and the assurance story in one artifact. Publish it, date it, badge it in the README. Almost nobody in this space has one.

Distribution: what is table stakes, and what is folklore

  • Registry presence in every language you claim. Downloads are the only unfakeable adoption number. Measured: litellm 708.5M/month on PyPI; mcp (Python) 324M; @modelcontextprotocol/sdk 200M npm; rmcp (Rust) ~3.2M. The Python-to-Rust ratio for the same protocol is roughly 100:1 &mdash; tier languages by that, not by symmetry.
  • A one-liner that is not a language package manager &mdash; curl|bash, npx or uvx &mdash; plus a single static binary on Releases and a container on ghcr.io.
  • Homebrew is folklore. cosign gets 4,501 brew installs per 30 days; OPA 1,149. Against 708M/month on PyPI that is rounding error. Ship it, do not prioritise it.

But for an agent-security substrate it is the cheapest differentiation available: actions/attest-build-provenance is free and makes gh attestation verify work out of the box.
  • llms.txt is an open gap in exactly your tier. Anthropic, Cloudflare, Stripe, LangChain, Vercel, Snyk, Daytona and E2B all serve one. garak, PyRIT and Guardrails AI all 404. The security-tooling tier has not shipped agent-facing docs, and it costs an afternoon.

The anti-pattern you are sitting in right now

Unpublished packages is a named failure mode; a documented-but-non-functional command is worse. For a security project the documentation is the security claim &mdash; and npx aumos-mcp --standalone printing nothing and exiting 0 will be found inside ninety seconds. Stars are recoverable; &ldquo;their docs lied about what runs&rdquo; is not. Fix or delete that command before anything is published.

03 · Portfolio

Which components create exponential value, and which are borrowed time

You asked for the honest verdict rather than the best available framing, so that is what this is. A component earns KEEP only if it does something a developer or an enterprise cannot get more cheaply elsewhere. The test is not &ldquo;is this good code&rdquo; &mdash; several CUT components are good code. The test is whether a security architect, asked why you built it instead of adopting the incumbent, would accept the answer.

Shown here are the components where the verdict is decisive in either direction. The full 54-entry treatment is in the critical analysis. Roughly 35&ndash;40 of the original 54 are commodity; this is the spine that survives the cut to ~25 plus the clearest deletions.

The pattern worth naming

Every CUT component fails the same way: it reimplements something a well-funded incumbent gives away, and the reimplementation is a fraction as complete. 04 · Capability surface

What else the portfolio can credibly offer beyond the four use cases

You asked what else these components can provide. Each of these is reachable from work that already exists, and each maps to a buyer who is not currently in your four use cases. They are ordered by distance from what is already built.

1. Model-file safety as a standalone product

gguf-ext already does bounded parsing with fuzz targets against a format with a live CVSS 9.8 CVE. Every team that loads community model files has this exposure and no safe option. Shippable as an independent crate/CLI in weeks, with its own audience, and it needs none of the rest of the stack.

2. Grader attestation for AI evaluation vendors

Nobody pins the grader. Eval platforms, benchmark maintainers and AI-assurance firms all have the same credibility problem: the entity producing the evidence is the entity being evaluated. A signed judge-and-corpus attestation is a product for the eval ecosystem itself, not just for end users.

3. Sovereign evidence retention

CERT-In requires 180 days of ICT logs held inside Indian jurisdiction; DPDP adds a one-year processing-log requirement. An evidence plane that is portable, air-gappable and residency-aware is a compliance product for India and the Gulf that a US-hosted control plane structurally cannot match.

4. Agent incident exchange for CERTs and ISACs

CERT-In Annexure I item (xx) already makes attacks on AI/ML systems reportable within 6 hours. Nobody has tooling that produces that report. OCSF 1.9 shipped the ai_agent and delegation objects to carry it. This is a narrow, unglamorous, genuinely unserved need.

5. Differential-privacy accounting as a service

dp-crate is the best-tested package in the repo. DP accounting appears in RBI FREE-AI and DPDP conversations, and most teams implement it wrong. A correct, provable accountant with published proofs is defensible on its own.

6. Conformance-as-a-service for the protocol suite

The conformance runner now executes 220 verifications across four languages and is genuinely fail-closed. If P8 or P12 gain any adoption, the conformance suite becomes the neutral arbiter &mdash; which is how standards bodies fund themselves and how a specification becomes load-bearing rather than aspirational.

The through-line

Five of those six are evidence products, not enforcement products. That is not a coincidence &mdash; it is the same conclusion the competitive analysis reaches from the other direction. The portfolio's centre of gravity should move from stopping agents to proving what agents did, because the first is commodity and the second is not.

05 · Developer adoption

Why a developer cannot start today — seven concrete blockers

These are ordered by how early a developer hits them. The first three are fatal within sixty seconds of arriving, and none of them is about code quality.

  • Nothing is published. All four npm packages 404. There is no PyPI project, no crate, no container image, no release binary. A developer's first action &mdash; npm install or pip install &mdash; fails. Everything else on this list is downstream of that. [EXECUTED]
  • There is no git remote. The repository has never been pushed, so there is no README to land on, no issues, no stars, no releases page. It is invisible.
  • The documented run command is a silent no-op. npx aumos-mcp --standalone prints nothing and exits 0 on every platform, because the main-module check compares an npm symlink against a resolved realpath. The one command in the README produces silence. [EXECUTED]
  • There is no SDK. For Claude Agent SDK, OpenAI Agents SDK, LangGraph, CrewAI, Pydantic AI or Vercel AI SDK there is no adapter, no middleware, no hook, no example. The core question &mdash; how do I wrap my existing agent loop? &mdash; has no answer in the repository. aumos_langchain is the single genuine adapter and it is not even a catalogued component.
  • The README's copy-paste config is dead on arrival. It ships "command": "npx", "args": ["-y", "@muveraai/aumos-mcp-server"] &mdash; a 404 &mdash; and sets AUMOS_MODE: standalone, which is the mode that returns hardcoded security passes. A developer who gets past the registry error lands on a control plane that always says yes.
  • Two competing namespaces. @aumos/ and @muveraai/ both appear in the tree, and the MCP server and gateway are split across them. Resolved by the move to warrantor, but it must actually be executed.
  • No trust signals. No audit, no fuzzing badge, no provenance attestation, no maintainer roster, no release cadence, no reference user. A developer evaluating a security library checks these before reading a line of code.
The blunt version

The repository contains 287 passing Rust tests, 227 TypeScript tests, a four-language conformance suite at 220/220, and genuinely excellent parser work &mdash; and a developer cannot execute a single line of it. The gap between engineering quality and adoption readiness is the largest single finding in this document, and it is entirely fixable with work that requires no cleverness at all.

07 · The regulatory wedge

The Gulf is four years ahead of the US, and nobody serves it

The finding that should reshape your positioning

CBUAE Model Management Standards (Nov 2022) &sect;2.4.1 Table 1 lists &ldquo;Artificial Intelligence&rdquo; as an in-scope model type, binding on all licensed UAE banks including Islamic institutions and foreign branches. UAE banks have owed model-risk artifacts on AI models since 2022. The US carved generative and agentic AI out of scope in April 2026. The Gulf is ahead of the US on binding AI model governance, and has been for four years. [EXTERNAL, primary]

The corrected picture is narrower and far more useful than &ldquo;no format exists anywhere&rdquo;: **no GCC regulator publishes a fillable template &mdash; but three of four prescribe the contents.** Contents without a container is exactly what a substrate can emit.

Data residency — the hard, examinable anchor for attested inference

  • UAE Outsourcing Regulation, Circular 14/2021, Art 6.1 &mdash; &ldquo;Banks must ensure that the Master System of Record, which includes all Confidential Data, is continuously maintained and stored within the UAE.&rdquo; No export without CBUAE approval plus written customer consent plus acknowledgement of foreign legal access.   • Saudi CSF &sect;3.4.3 &mdash; &ldquo;in principle only cloud services should be used that are located in Saudi Arabia,&rdquo; with SAMA approval required before signing the contract, not after.
  • No hosting-attestation form exists in either jurisdiction. The bank assembles the register entry, the non-objection letter and the contract clauses itself. A substrate that emits a hardware-rooted attestation of where inference actually executed fills a gap the regulators created and did not fill.

India moves the opposite way from the US, eight weeks later

RBI's Draft Guidance on Regulatory Principles for Model Risk Management, 24 June 2026 (consultation closed 24 July) defines model expansively &mdash; &para;7(3) covers &ldquo;algorithms, analytics, interfaces, applications, decision-based rules and other computational tools&hellip; irrespective of whether such tools are recognised as models by the RE&rdquo; &mdash; explicitly scopes foundational and frontier AI models (&para;49), and adds &ldquo;level of autonomy placed on model outputs&rdquo; to risk tiering (&para;52). The US carved agentic AI out on 17 April 2026; India scoped it in on 24 June 2026. That is the cleanest dated regulatory contrast available anywhere. [EXTERNAL, primary]

Two architectural requirements that are cheap now and expensive later

1. The evidence store must be independently locatable per tenant. CERT-In requires 180 days of logs held inside Indian jurisdiction; CBUAE requires the Master System of Record in the UAE; SAMA requires In-Kingdom by default. RBI &para;23 wants decommissioned-model records for 10 years. If the architecture assumes one global evidence store, that is a blocker in three of four target jurisdictions &mdash; and it is far cheaper to fix before a pilot than after.

2. Timestamps need a recorded, traceable time source. CERT-In direction (i) requires clock synchronisation to NIC or NPL servers. Evidence whose timestamps are not traceable to those sources is weak under that regime. Make the time source configurable and record which source signed the timestamp &mdash; a small change in the evidence record format, and a retrofit if left.

The commercial wedge, stated plainly

Four jurisdictions want structurally different evidence packs from the same underlying system &mdash; Qatar wants register fields, the UAE wants nine-component validation reports and committee minutes, Saudi wants a 0&ndash;5 maturity score, DIFC wants processing-purpose logs and algorithmic audits. Nobody has solved emitting all of them from one telemetry layer &mdash; not the regulators, and not Microsoft, whose compliance mapping targets EU AI Act, HIPAA and SOC 2.

This is a better positioning than anything else in this document. It is defensible without claiming novel enforcement technology, it is a problem the incumbent does not address, and it turns the multi-region requirement from a burden into the product.

And one hard blocker you need to act on

Qatar QCB &sect;10.7.5 requires the AI provider's LEI, country of registration, local corporate registration number, registered address, parent company and the governing law of the licensing arrangement &mdash; plus a named alternate provider and a substitutability rating for high-risk systems. An unincorporated open-source project cannot populate that register. For Qatar, a legal entity is a precondition of deployment, not a production nice-to-have. This is the clearest instance found anywhere of the &ldquo;no vendor entity&rdquo; risk becoming a hard regulatory blocker rather than a soft procurement preference.

08 · The thesis

Where the exponential actually is

Exponential value does not come from covering more surface than Microsoft. It comes from owning a primitive that becomes load-bearing for other people's systems. Three candidates survive scrutiny, and they compound.

1. Be the evidence layer, not the enforcement layer

Enforcement is commodity and getting more so &mdash; Microsoft AGT, AWS AgentCore Policy at GA in 13 regions, Envoy AI Gateway v1.0, Cedar, OPA. Verifiable evidence is not. Nobody signs an eval. Nobody pins the grader. OCC 2026-13 explicitly puts generative and agentic AI out of scope while telling banks their own governance &ldquo;should guide the determination of appropriate controls&rdquo; &mdash; a supervisor saying you own this gap.

The compounding move: P8 bundles become the thing an auditor asks for by name. Once one institution accepts a signed eval bundle as evidence, the format has a reference &mdash; and references are how formats become standards.

2. Be the conformance authority for the protocols you define

You already run 220 cross-language verifications, fail-closed. Sigstore proves the pattern: a public dated conformance suite lets a 90-star library credibly call itself production-ready, and makes the suite the neutral arbiter. If P8 or P12 gain adoption, whoever runs the conformance suite defines what compliance means &mdash; a durable position no amount of Microsoft engineering displaces.

3. Be the sovereign, multi-jurisdiction evidence emitter

The one advantage a US-hosted control plane structurally cannot match, and now the best-evidenced. CERT-In requires 180 days of ICT logs inside Indian jurisdiction and makes AI/ML attacks reportable within six hours &mdash; today. CBUAE requires the Master System of Record inside the UAE. SAMA requires in-Kingdom cloud by default with approval before contract signature. And the four Gulf jurisdictions each demand a different evidence pack from the same system.

Portability plus multi-jurisdiction emission is the product. sovereign-stack is currently the least-invested component relative to its strategic weight &mdash; the clearest misallocation in the portfolio.

What to say when a partner asks “why not the Microsoft one?”

&ldquo;Use it &mdash; for enforcement. It is good, it is free, and we interoperate with it. What it does not give you is an artifact your auditor can verify without trusting your runtime, or a deployment your regulator will accept inside national boundaries, or one telemetry layer that emits a QCB AI Register entry, a CBUAE nine-component validation report and a DIFC processing-purpose log from the same run. That is what we do.&rdquo; True, checkable, and it converts the competitor into a dependency rather than a rival.
