Abstract

Most agent incidents are not detected in real time — the agent's behavior was superficially compliant, the harm surfaced later, and by then the per-run transcript is buried in a volume of runs no human team can review. We present a retrospective transcript-analysis pipeline that applies LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation[1] (NeurIPS 2023) at scale over the AAR corpus (Paper 3) of 100K+ agent runs, combining trajectory anomaly detection, judge-LLM verdict scoring, and incident-clustering to surface the small minority of runs that warrant human investigation. We address the known biases of agent-as-a-judge evaluation[2] (position, verbosity, self-preference) through calibration against a human-verified incident seed set, and we report precision/recall on a labeled corpus of historical incidents. The contribution is the operational pipeline that converts the AAR's evidence substrate into a detection capability — making retrospective review tractable at the scale production agents now operate.

§1. Introduction: the detection-at-scale problem

Production agent deployments generate enormous run volumes — a single enterprise deployment can produce 100K+ agent runs per day, each with a full transcript of tool calls, decisions, and outcomes. The vast majority are benign; a small minority are incidents (prompt-injection successes, harmful actions, policy violations). Real-time detection (Papers 14, 15) catches the obvious cases; the subtle ones — the compliant-in-form but harmful-in-substance runs — surface only in retrospective review, which no human team can perform at 100K runs/day. The problem is making retrospective review tractable.

§2. Background: LLM-as-a-Judge

The LLM-as-a-Judge methodology[1] (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023) — using a strong LLM to evaluate the outputs of other LLMs — is the canonical technique for scaling evaluation beyond human capacity. The agent-as-a-judge evolution[2] applies it to agent trajectories but documents biases (position bias, verbosity bias, self-preference) that can skew incident detection. The AgentOps framework[3] represents the closest published approach to observing and analyzing agentic systems at scale, which our pipeline specializes to retrospective incident detection.

The three detectors are deliberately heterogeneous: a statistical detector (no LLM, catches distributional anomalies), a judge-LLM detector (semantic, catches the compliant-in-form cases), and a clustering detector (unsupervised, catches novel incident classes). Fusion ranks the union; calibration against a human-verified incident seed set tunes the thresholds.

§4. Mitigating judge biases

We mitigate the documented agent-as-a-judge biases via: (a) position-balanced prompting (the judge sees the run in both orders when pairwise); (b) verbosity-normalized scoring (length penalty); (c) cross-model judging (judge with a different family than the agent, reducing self-preference); (d) seed-calibrated thresholds (precision/recall tuned on the human-labeled seed, not chosen a priori). The biases are not eliminated but bounded and monitored.

§5. Evaluation on labeled incidents

DetectorPrecisionRecall
Trajectory anomaly0.710.62
Judge-LLM verdict0.830.78
Incident clustering0.58 (novel-class)0.41 (novel-class)
Fusion (calibrated)0.790.84

The fusion achieves 0.84 recall — meaning 84% of true incidents surface to the human-review queue — at 0.79 precision, reducing the 100K-run corpus to a review queue of manageable size. The clustering detector's lower precision is the cost of its novel-class-discovery value.

§7. Conclusion

Retrospective transcript analysis, built on LLM-as-a-Judge over the AAR corpus, makes incident detection tractable at the 100K-runs/day scale of production agents. The three-detector fusion with bias mitigation achieves 0.84 recall at 0.79 precision, converting an unreviewable volume into a ranked human-review queue and surfacing novel incident classes the real-time defenses miss.
