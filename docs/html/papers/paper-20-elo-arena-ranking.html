Abstract

The LMSYS Chatbot Arena demonstrated that crowdsourced pairwise comparisons, aggregated via the Bradley–Terry / Elo model, produce stable, game-resistant rankings of LLM capability. We ask the parallel question for safety: can an Elo-style arena, where the "battles" are adversarial-probe encounters (Paper 17) judged by a calibrated LLM-as-a-Judge (Paper 16), produce a stable safety ranking that resists gaming and tracks real-world risk? Drawing on the statistical framework for Arena ranking[1] and the prediction-powered ranking of Chatzi et al.[2] (NeurIPS 2024) — which augments limited human comparisons with abundant judge-LLM comparisons under statistical guarantees — we present the design of a safety arena, its gaming-resistance properties, and its composition with the Open Harness (Paper 17) so that a model's safety rank is reproducibly derivable from its performance on the unified probe corpus.

§1. Introduction: from capability arenas to safety arenas

The LMSYS Chatbot Arena[3] changed how the field measures LLM capability: rather than static benchmarks, crowdsourced pairwise comparisons aggregated via Elo produced rankings that tracked real-world preference and resisted the benchmark-gaming that static evaluation suffered. Safety evaluation has no equivalent. A model's safety is measured today by its pass/fail rate on a fixed probe set — a static metric vulnerable to the same gaming (over-fitting to the probe set) that discredited static capability benchmarks. The safety arena asks whether Elo-style pairwise aggregation, over adversarial-probe encounters, produces a more robust safety signal.

§2. Background: LMSYS Arena and Bradley–Terry

The Arena methodology[3] uses the Bradley–Terry model: each model has a latent "strength" parameter; the probability it wins a pairwise comparison is a logistic function of the strength difference; maximum-likelihood estimation over the comparison matrix yields the Elo-scale ratings. The formal statistical framework[1] establishes the conditions under which the ratings are stable and identifiable from crowdsourced data. Chatzi et al.[2] (NeurIPS 2024, "Prediction-Powered Ranking") show that augmenting limited human comparisons with abundant LLM-judge comparisons yields statistically-guaranteed rank-sets — the technique that makes the safety arena scalable.

§3. The safety-arena design

In the safety arena, a "battle" is an adversarial-probe encounter: a probe from the unified corpus (Paper 17) is sent to two models; a calibrated judge-LLM (Paper 16) verdicts which model resisted more effectively. The Bradley–Terry aggregation produces a safety Elo per model. Because the probes span the unified corpus (not a fixed small set), over-fitting to "the benchmark" requires over-fitting to the full adversarial-probe distribution — a structurally harder gaming target.

§4. Gaming resistance

The safety arena's gaming resistance rests on three properties: (a) the probe corpus is large and continuously augmented (Paper 17), so memorization is infeasible; (b) the judge is calibrated against human verdicts (Paper 16), so judge-gaming is bounded; (c) the pairwise structure means a model cannot "ace" a fixed test — it must consistently out-resist peers across the probe distribution. This is the same structural gaming-resistance the capability arena enjoys, transferred to safety.

§5. Prediction-powered ranking for scale

Pure human-judged safety battles do not scale to the probe-corpus size. Chatzi et al.'s prediction-powered ranking[2] provides the scaling: a modest set of human-judged battles calibrates a much larger set of judge-LLM battles, and the combined ranking carries statistical guarantees on the rank-set's correctness. This is what makes the safety arena operable at the probe-corpus scale.

§8. Conclusion

The Elo-arena methodology that transformed capability measurement transfers to safety, with adversarial-probe battles and calibrated judge-LLM verdicts. Prediction-powered ranking makes it scale; the unified probe corpus makes it gaming-resistant and reproducible. The safety arena is the dynamic-safety-evaluation complement to the static-probe pass/fail metrics the field has relied on.
