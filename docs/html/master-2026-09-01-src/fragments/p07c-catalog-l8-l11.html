How the four strata fit the spine

The first two catalog parts covered the strata that stop an unauthorized action and record an authorized one. These four cover what happens around that core. L8, the human surface, is where a person approves, denies, halts, contests or reads. L9, model intelligence, is the platform's own set of small guard models (a guard model is a classifier that reads an action or a message and returns a verdict such as allow, flag or deny) and the pipeline that trains them from the platform's own records. L10, federation and sovereignty, is what lets a second organization, a supervisor or an insurer verify a record without trusting the first organization's servers. L11, change, configuration and lifecycle governance, is the plane the incident could not teach, because agents, models, policies, connectors and schemas all change independently and the July attacker never filed a change request.

Three rules from the standing doctrine bind all four. A guard model is advisory and never the enforcement point (the blueprint's rule and the OS specification's fourth anti-goal) S62 §13.2. Nothing here introduces a new trust root: every federation item is built as the weaker local version that establishes less and says so S62 §13.1. And the largest reservoir of unrealized value is the human surface: the buyer class that funds the platform cannot use it without a developer, which the OS specification calls a market-access gap rather than a usability gap S62 §7 L8.

L8 · Human surface PROPOSED

A receipt is the signed record the platform writes for every authorized action; a warrant is the signed grant of authority an agent runs under. Neither is useful to a risk officer, an auditor or a supervisor unless a surface renders it, routes it and lets a person act on it. The OS specification lists thirteen first-pass items and seven second-pass items for that surface; the blueprint's Plane H adds the value instrumentation (exposure budgets, oversight yield, safe-autonomy expansion, board packs) that says whether the governance is worth its cost S61 Plane H. This part reconciles both and adds the natural-language console and customer trust center from the build catalogue S64 D-1, K. The stratum serves the W4 policy compiler, the component that turns a written policy into something the substrate can enforce.

Two facts shape every item. First, an approval path slower than the agent is a path that gets designed around, so latency is a security property here and the scoreboard carries approval latency as metric 11 with approval quality as its counter-metric S62 §14. Second, the human-in-the-loop is itself a control that can fail: the blueprint names "review becomes rubber-stamping" as a strategy-level risk and specifies risk routing, queue service levels, sampling, disagreement metrics, reason quality, reviewer rotation and no incentive tied to approval rate as the countermeasure S61 §20. The regulatory anchors that lead are the human-override expectation in India's lending guidance S41, the audit-rights and immediate-shutdown expectations in the Gulf S43, and the US model-risk deferral, which removed the framework that would have specified these controls rather than the duty to have them S39.

L11 · Change, configuration & lifecycle governance PROPOSED

The plane the incident could not have taught, because the July attacker never filed a change request, never triggered a rollback and never ran under a version anyone had pinned. Agents, models, policies, connectors and schemas change independently, and "it worked once" is not lifecycle control. OpenAI's own security lesson says the same thing from the other side: "Accidental security gaps are often introduced due to human error, the combination of several seemingly unrelated changes over days or weeks, or due to lack of security context when making a change in a system that may not initially seem to be relevant to security" First-party · OpenAI S01 §VII.C.

Design rule

A catalog derived from one incident is strong where that incident was strong and blind everywhere it was quiet. Nothing was missing from the authority, egress, evidence-integrity or containment-detection families, the areas July stressed hardest, and almost everything was missing from lifecycle, human factors, exceptions, recovery and rights. Any future catalog derived from a single event should be diffed against an operations-derived portfolio before anyone calls it complete S62 §7.12.

In plain words

Parts 07a and 07b describe how the platform stops an agent doing what it was not authorized to do and proves what it did. This part describes how a person uses that, how the platform's own small models get better without ever being trusted to decide, how a second organization or a supervisor checks the record without trusting the first organization's computers, and how the whole thing keeps being true after the next release, the next configuration change and the next regulation. Thirty-two of the seventy-eight items here were not in the OS specification; every one names the document it came from, and none of them exists yet.
