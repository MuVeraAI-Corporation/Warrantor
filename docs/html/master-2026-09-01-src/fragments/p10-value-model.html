Part 10

The exponential value model, the outcomes worth funding, and the scoreboard with its counter-metrics

This part states what compounds and what merely costs money, reconciles three separate lists of value loops into one canonical set of ten, and publishes the scoreboard twice over: once as the metrics that say how much governance is happening, and once as the counter-metrics that say whether it is worth its cost. One loop of the ten has evidence that it turns. The other nine are proposals with a named test.

The value equation

The blueprint states the commercial objective as a ratio rather than a feature list: a materially higher volume of productive autonomous work set against the human oversight, incident exposure, audit effort and duplicated governance it costs to run that work safely. Internal S61 Written as a formula in words, the numerator is governed autonomous work completed at accepted quality — work units an owner agreed in advance to count, delivered inside declared limits. The denominator is the sum of four costs: reviewer hours consumed, expected loss from incidents, hours spent assembling evidence for audits, and the cost of building the same control twice because two teams could not share one.

Five terms move that ratio, and it is worth being exact about which of them is a cost reduction and which is a capability. Safe autonomy raises the numerator: bounded authority — permission that is explicitly scoped, time-limited and revocable rather than ambient — lets an organization run work it would otherwise refuse to run at all. Lower oversight cost, faster evidence and reusable controls all attack the denominator: one control and evidence shape replacing fragmented prompts and review conventions; proof generated as work happens rather than reconstructed afterward; policies, approvals and mappings reused across agents, teams and jurisdictions. Measured trust is the term that makes the other four checkable — a living signal tied to outcomes rather than a static compliance claim. Internal S61

In plain words

A receipt is a signed record of what an agent was authorized to do and what it actually did, written by the system rather than by the agent. A warrant is the grant of authority that record is issued against. Egress means outbound network access — the destinations a workload can reach. A counter-metric is a second number you watch specifically because the cheapest way to improve the first number is to break the second one.

The exponential claim, stated precisely

"Exponential value" is used loosely enough that the specification defines it narrowly. A feature qualifies as exponential only if it closes a loop whose output increases the value of every previously shipped feature. A feature that serves one more customer is linear. A feature that makes every existing receipt more valuable to every existing holder is exponential. Internal S62 §1 Everything else in the catalog is tagged linear and sequenced on dependency instead, because a catalog where everything is exponential is a catalog where nothing is.

Four things compound in this architecture, and they compound for structural reasons rather than commercial ones. A guarantee set that only tightens: every attack that succeeds against an invariant becomes a permanent conformance test, so the set of properties a deployment inherits grows monotonically and never regresses. Evidence reused across audiences: one signed trace serves the responder, the engineer, the auditor, the customer, the insurer, the supervisor and the researcher without anyone rebuilding custody. Internal S65 §11 Policy reuse: an approved use case becomes a parameterized template, so the next use case starts narrower and is approved faster. The attack-to-policy ratchet: a verified finding in evaluation becomes a runtime denial in production, with the linkage itself receipted.

What the claim excludes matters as much. It is not a claim about growth rates and not a revenue projection: no number in this part forecasts adoption, market share or revenue. Four families of work are valuable, several urgent, and none compound: packaging, signing and installers, which gate distribution but raise the value of nothing already shipped; a second dashboard for an audience that already has one, which is linear in users and sublinear once maintenance is counted; support for one more model provider, which is additive; and performance work below the threshold at which latency changes behavior. Internal S62 §6.3 That last one has an edge: above the threshold it is a loop input, because an approval path slower than the agent is an approval path that gets designed around.

Design rule

The honest test for a loop, applied to every entry in the table below: name a measurable input that increases a measurable output that in turn increases the input, and name the decay term — the mechanism by which the loop slows, leaks or reverses if nobody maintains it. A loop stated without its decay term is a slogan. A loop whose decay term cannot be measured is not yet a loop.

The loops, reconciled

Three internal documents state loop inventories independently and they do not agree on count, naming or granularity. The specification names six with growth terms and turn rates Internal S62 §6.1; the blueprint names ten, each with a cold-start strategy and an anti-gaming control S61; the research source names eight created by the platform extension S65 §11. Twenty-four loop statements, heavy overlap, no canonical list.

They reconcile to ten. The specification's six survive intact and keep their identifiers, because the build catalog already tags every item with one of them and renumbering would silently break several hundred cross-references. Four more are added from the blueprint and the research source because they have no home in the six and are not reducible to them: decision reuse, exception economics, population detection baselines, and outcome measurement. Two research loops are folded rather than kept separate, and the folding is a judgment worth stating: defender access is a delivery mode for the assurance ratchet and the verification network rather than a loop of its own, and training integrity is the honesty gate inside the data flywheel, not a parallel cycle — its catalog item is the signed checkpoint disposition record. Internal S62 §7.12

One structural fact falls out of the reconciliation and it is not flattering. Only L1 through L6 carry a loop tag anywhere in the build catalog. The four added loops — the ones the blueprint reasoned to from operations rather than from the incident — have no tagged items, so the catalog cannot currently answer "what would close this loop" for four of the ten. The one loop that is already proven, and what it tells us

L1 is not a projection, and it is the only entry above that is not. The pipeline has run the whole chain: corpora were built from graph data, five fine-tuning runs were trained across a local accelerator and four cloud accounts in parallel, a parity gate was applied, and the run ended with one promotion and four recorded refusals. Internal S62 §6.2 The cheapest turn of the loop was measured: a domain corpus on a small model, recall improved by 6.7 points with a better false-positive rate, on roughly thirty minutes of rented accelerator time costing single-digit dollars. Internal S64 §2

Be exact about what that establishes, because it is easy to overstate in both directions. It establishes that the mechanism runs and that the marginal cost of a turn is measured in dollars rather than headcount — which is the property that makes the loop compound at all. It does not establish that the resulting models are production controls. A later review found that every evaluated fine-tune damaged severity behavior and that recall-only review had been insufficient; the standing decision is that these models remain advisory, with schema-validity, severity-stability, calibration, slice and paired-outcome gates required before any production promotion. Internal S61 The correct reading is not that the flywheel failed. It is that the flywheel turned, the gate caught what a weaker gate would have shipped, and the negative result was retained. That is the ratchet of L5 operating on L1, which is what the reconciliation above predicts.

The refusals matter more than the promotion strategically. Negative results are corpus too, and they are the part a competitor cannot synthesize: a rival can copy the architecture in a quarter, but not four documented experiments showing which recipes fail on which categories, because those cost accelerator hours against a corpus that exists only downstream of a running platform. Internal S62 §6.2

Finding

The flywheel's failure mode is a promotion that should have been a refusal, and two measurement rules learned expensively must be enforced by the pipeline rather than held in memory. First, a point-estimate ordering is not a finding: a monotonicity check fires on any decrease, and one such check reported noise at p ≈ 0.69 as a reversal. Second, every quoted figure carries the configuration that produced it — context length, seed and quantization each move the number, and a recall figure was once quoted at a context length the product did not run at. Automating the loop without automating both gates converts the moat into a liability. Internal S62 §6.2

Eight outcomes worth funding

Features are means. These eight outcomes are the investment units, and each is stated with four things: the value mechanism, the leading indicators that move first, the outcome metric that decides whether it worked, and the counter-metric that prevents the platform from "improving" by shifting risk or labor somewhere it is not measured. Internal S61 The baseline for each is owned by the customer: a baseline we supply is a marketing number, one the customer agreed before the work started is evidence.

O1 · Adoption capacity

Run more useful autonomous work safely

Expand the number, scope and consequence level of autonomous workflows an organization is willing to operate. Leading: governed agents, approved use cases, mediated action classes, time to authorization. Outcome: completed autonomous work units and business throughput per reviewer hour. Counter: incidents, silent bypass, false refusals, accumulated held work, rubber-stamp approvals. Catalog: L8-20 outcome contracts, L11-01 shadow-agent discovery, L4-10…L4-18 adapters. S61

O2 · Oversight economics

Decouple supervision cost from agent count

Standardize evidence, approval, exceptions and review so the twentieth agent is cheaper to govern than the first. Leading: template reuse, auto-resolved routine work, queue aging, evidence completeness. Outcome: median review minutes per consequential action and per active agent. Counter: reviewer fatigue, approval concentration, reopened decisions, manual evidence work. Catalog: L8-01 approval queue, L8-15 partial approval, L8-17 approval-quality controls. S61

O3 · Delivery velocity

Shorten time from agent idea to governed production

Replace bespoke security review with reusable manifests, policies, tests, deployment patterns and pre-authorization evidence. Leading: days to inventory, classify, approve, integrate and pass conformance. Outcome: time-to-first-governed-value and change lead time. Counter: exceptions, control debt, policy divergence, failed rollbacks. Catalog: L8-04 policy studio, L8-12 non-developer onboarding, L11-04 change and rollback governance. S61

O4 · Assurance on demand

Generate evidence at the speed of work

Continuously transform signed operational proof into auditor, customer, supervisor, board and incident-response views. Leading: evidence coverage, verification success, control freshness, mapping reuse. Outcome: audit preparation hours, questionnaire cycle time, time to prove an incident boundary. Counter: unsupported claims, stale mappings, unverifiable receipts, evidence with ambiguous custody. Catalog: L8-09 crosswalk engine, L8-10 evidence packs, L7-06 completeness attestation. S61

O5 · Loss containment

Reduce blast radius and recovery time

Bound authority, destinations, credentials, lifetime, delegation and commit; detect drift; reach a known-safe state. Leading: containment coverage, revocation latency, safe-state tests, escape-suite results. Outcome: exposure duration, affected assets, median time to containment and to recovery. Counter: fail-open events, recovery side effects, service-outage amplification, untested safe states. Catalog: L0-04 egress-closure prover, L4-21 recovery orchestration, L4-22 break-glass warrants. O6 · Spend productivity

Make agent cost attributable and governable

The scoreboard, and the half it was missing

Seventeen metrics. The first fourteen were chosen because each is hard to game, maps to a stratum or a loop, and would have been non-trivially bad during the incident. A platform that publishes these is making a checkable claim; one that publishes architecture diagrams is not. Internal S62 §14

The correction that produced the rest of this section is worth stating plainly, because it changes the shape of the scoreboard rather than adding to it. Every one of the original fourteen measures how much governance is happening. Not one measures whether it is worth its cost. A platform optimizing only that column improves its numbers by adding friction, and the metric that would have caught it did not exist. Internal S62 §14.1 This is the same failure the specification names as the most probable way the whole thing dies — not an attack, but a control-plane tax: if the platform adds latency, review burden and operating overhead faster than it enables autonomous work, teams route around it, and a control that is routed around provides negative security, because it also carries the belief that something is governed. Internal S62 §13.1 It is the mechanism behind the analyst finding reported in secondary coverage that up to 40% of enterprises demote or decommission agents after production incidents. Analyst · licensed-source check pending S45

So every metric now carries two companions: a counter-metric, which is the thing the cheapest way of moving the metric would break, and a cost-worth metric, which prices the governance the metric is measuring. A counter-metric moving the wrong way outranks its metric moving the right way. Nine of the seventeen counter-metrics below are as the specification states them; the counter-metrics for rows 3, 4, 7, 9, 10, 15, 16 and 17 and all seventeen cost-worth metrics are proposed here and are not yet in any published scoreboard. Friction and false-denial budgets

Two counter-metrics only work if they are budgeted rather than minimized, which is the least intuitive discipline in the scoreboard. A false-denial budget is a stated allowance for legitimate work the boundary refuses — the price of the guarantee, agreed per workflow and spent deliberately. Driving it to zero means the boundary binds on nothing. A friction budget is the same idea for latency and review load: an agreed ceiling on how much slower governed work may be than ungoverned work, with a breach treated as a defect in the control. The instruction that follows is to measure time-to-value, reviewer load, bypass rate and false-denial budget per workflow, and to retire any control whose benefit is not evidenced — a platform that cannot name a control it has killed is not measuring itself. Internal S62 §13.1

Oversight yield is the metric that reconciles the two columns, and it is the one to put in front of a board. Prevented loss plus accepted outcome value, over reviewer minutes. Internal S65 §10 It rises when governance gets cheaper and when it gets more effective, and it falls when either the numerator is inflated by unfalsifiable prevention claims or the denominator is hidden by pushing review onto people nobody counts. Both failure modes are visible in the counter-metric column above, which is the point of publishing them together.
