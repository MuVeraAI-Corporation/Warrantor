Executive Summary

The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689[1]), fully applicable from 2 August 2026, imposes the world's first binding obligations on providers and deployers of AI systems — most consequentially for general-purpose AI models (Article 53) and models presenting systemic risk (Article 55), with the GPAI Code of Practice[2] providing the presumption-of-conformity pathway. The legal text is precise about what must be done but silent on how, leaving enterprise CISOs to translate statutory language into engineering controls and evidence. This whitepaper is that translation: a clause-by-clause mapping of Articles 9–13, 53, and 55 to specific AumOS components, configurations, and the machine-verifiable evidence artifacts each obligation produces.

§1. The practitioner problem

A CISO responsible for a GPAI system deployed in the EU faces a compliance gap that traditional GRC tooling cannot close. The Act demands, for example, "detailed, up-to-date technical documentation" (Art. 11), "automatic logs" (Art. 12(2)), "post-market monitoring" (Art. 72), and for systemic-risk models "adversarial testing ... to identify and mitigate systemic risks" (Art. 55(1)(c)). Each requires a technical artifact that is tamper-evident, exportable to the AI Office on demand, and bound to a specific model version. The penalty for non-compliance — up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover (Art. 99) — makes the gap existential.

§3. Methodology: evidence by construction

The defining design choice is that compliance evidence is produced by construction — as an automatic by-product of operating the system — rather than reconstructed under audit pressure. Every model release generates its ML-BOM; every authorized agent action generates its receipt; every red-team run generates its verdict. The CISO's audit response is not a six-week scramble; it is the export of already-existing, already-tamper-evident artifacts (P8 audit export). This shifts compliance from a periodic cost center to a continuous, low-friction property.

§4. Deployment sequence

  1. Inventory & classify. Identify high-risk (Annex III) or GPAI systems; classify systemic-risk models (≥10²⁵ FLOP, Art. 51).
  2. Documentation pipeline. Deploy S4/P10/P11; require ML-BOM + trust manifest per release (Articles 11, 53).
  3. Logging pipeline. Deploy flight-recorder (P2); anchor the receipt chain (Article 12, 55(1)(c)).
  4. Adversarial testing. Deploy Open Harness (X3); schedule red-team in CI; document methodology (Article 55(1)(a), Article 9).
  5. Post-market monitoring. Wire canaries to incident pipeline + kill switch (R3); document escalation (Article 72).
  6. AI-Office export bundle. Configure P8 to assemble, on demand, the complete artifact set for a system/period.

§5. What this produces for the regulator

A single, machine-verifiable bundle per system per period, containing the ML-BOM, the lineage proofs, the action-receipt chain, the red-team verdicts, and the incident records — all signed, all tamper-evident, all bound to the specific model versions in service. The AI Office can verify integrity without re-running the system. This is the structural difference between claiming compliance and demonstrating it.
